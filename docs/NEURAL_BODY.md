# A learned body under the mind

Status **proposed** (design and survey, 2026-11-26, Spark; the general coordinator approved a narrow CPU-only pilot of §7 the same day). The proposal is a small policy that turns a character's perceptions and the mind's current intent into player inputs at 10–15 Hz. Start with one melee duel, one compact CPU policy and a graph-based plan whose selected leaves can use the learned executor. A successful trial would show better timing and spacing under the same rules while the fighter still pursues its goal. The LLM remains responsible for goals, commitments, and what the character makes of its experience.

## Built (pilot plumbing)

Status: **built, off by default** (2026-11-26, Spark, branch `neural-body-pilot` from `origin/main` `8948974bc`, `fa2dbc3a6` merged in; not merged to main). The §7 pilot's plumbing only: a delegating graph leaf, the brief, body sessions on the player input path, a controller worker in `living/mind` with a scripted placeholder policy, the capture format and the leakage tests. No trained net, no reinforcement learning, no personal adapters, no Colab, no GPU, no model calls. Nothing here runs while the law `body_delegate_on` is off: a `delegate` leaf is refused at install, the new tables stay empty and the tick, perception and prompts are as on main (det_compare below).

**The law.** `body_delegate_on` (false), with `body_lease_ms` (750) and `body_brief_max_s` (300): value-only (`ValueOnly::Delivery`), read at the next install, reducer call or tick. The shipped script grows by 251 bytes; with a test overlay it now sits a few hundred bytes under the 256 KiB source cap (`script::body::tests::the_shipped_parts_joined_are_the_law_before_the_split`).

**The leaf** (`living_rules::graph::Node::Delegate`, option §3(c)):

```json
{"delegate": {"goal": "engage", "target": {"id": 21}, "skills": ["strike", "dodge"],
              "enter": {"sees": {"id": 21}}, "exit": {"health": {"below": 15}}}}
```

- `goal` is one of `engage`, `hold`, `withdraw` (`DELEGATE_GOALS`); `skills` is the scope, at most 8 (empty: movement only); `target`, `enter` and `exit` are an ordinary target and conditions. Unknown fields are refused, so no cue-to-action recipe can be written into it.
- Evaluation (`brain.rs` `Ev::delegate`): the leaf fails while its law is off, for a played character, when its target is not perceived, or, before it runs, while `enter` does not hold; it is done when `exit` holds; otherwise it runs and starts nothing itself. Its target and conditions read the character's own perception, as any leaf's.
- While it runs, the leaf stands in `body_slot`; the body controller's activities (node `act::PLAYER`) are not cancelled by the graph's after-step, and the leaf stays the plan's running step. When it ends (exit, a higher branch, a new graph), the slot goes and the body's held inputs are let go (`delegation::end_delegation`); a committed move runs its course.
- Refused at install (`mind::set_graph`, `set_routines`, `mind_patch`) with "a `delegate` leaf cannot be installed: in this world a plan carries out its own steps (the body_delegate_on law is off)", and for a played character with its own words.

**Tables** (new, private: reducers and the owner read them; keyed by character; nothing appended to an existing table):

| Table | Written | Read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `body_brief` (actor, revision, goal, target, stance, risk, style, traits, rationale, written_ms, expires_ms) | `mind_body_brief`, on replace or clear | the worker (typed values; never the rationale), the owning mind (`_body`) |
| `body_slot` (actor, revision, node, role, goal, target, skills, label, since_ms) | the character's evaluation, only on a change | input admission (`delegation::input_for`), the worker, the owning mind |
| `body_session` (actor, identity unique, controller, epoch, live, lease_ms btree, opened_ms, beat_ms) | `body_open`, `body_beat`, accepted inputs, the lease pass, disconnects | the player reducers, the viewer lists, `perception::current` |

All three are deleted at death (`act::forget_dead`). An expired brief is inert: its row is the character's one revision.

**Reducers.**

- `mind_body_brief(actor, expected, Option<BodyBriefIn>)`: replace (`Some`) or clear (`None`). Checked in order: the law; the caller controls a living mind-driven character ("not your character"); `expected` is the current revision ("the brief is at revision 3, not 2: read it again"); the schema and finite bounded values (`living_rules::body::check`: goal, stance `guarded`/`even`/`pressing`, risk and style 0–1, `traits` at most 8 values from -1 to 1, `ttl_s` 1 to `body_brief_max_s`, rationale at most 400 bytes); a target the character perceives now (`brain::resolve_for`), with the same words for a hidden creature and an id that does not exist ("#7 is not someone you perceive now"). A refusal is the reducer's error and writes nothing.
- `body_open(actor, body)`: the controller binds an identity of the body controller's own to the character (not its own, not the admin's, not a player's). The epoch rises; a session still live lapses first. The identity is swept at once (`perception::sweep_viewer_now`).
- `body_close(actor)`, `body_beat(epoch)`.
- The player input reducers `human_move`, `human_face`, `use_skill` and `release_skill` take a body session's identity too (`delegation::input_for`): the caller's played character first, else the live session's character while a leaf is delegated, a pressed skill within the leaf's `skills`. Every check, the input bucket, costs, timing and outcomes are the player's; a body's skill uses are recorded with source `body`. A person taking the controls replaces the plan with waiting; a body controller does not (`Driver::Body`). A body's movement deferred behind a rooted move resumes as a player's does (`combat::resume_move`).

**Perception.** A live session's identity is a player-equivalent viewer of its character: `combat::viewers` (people's characters, then live sessions) feeds the sweep, frames and the combat feed; `perception::current` answers the player views (`my_body`, `my_fighter`, `my_readiness`, `my_skill_uses`, `my_combat`, `seen_*`) with the session's character. `combat::human` still means a person (experience lines, heed's player side and memory are unchanged). No lines are staged for a session.

**Lease, stall and return.** The tick (`delegation::tick`, before the sweep) lapses sessions whose lease ran out (a btree range on `lease_ms`): the held direction stops, a held guard is let go, a movement waiting on a rooted move is dropped, its perception rows go, and committed moves settle under their rules. That is a key release, the input a player's client would send; the authority does not expire a disconnected player's held direction today, so this is the nearest equivalent and is stated as such. The delegated leaf keeps running, so the character stands: no graph takeover. The lease alone ends a session (not a closed connection: every HTTP call is a connection of its own). With the law turned off, every live session lapses and every slot ends at the next tick.

**The owning mind** reads its scene's `_body` (`delegation::scene_note`; never while the law is off), rendered by the mind service as "# Your body" (`prompts::body_section`): the accepted brief, its revision and time left, the rationale it gave, the delegated step and how long it has run, and "Your body is not answering now: you stand until it does" while no session holds it.

**The worker** (`living-mind body`, `living/mind/src/body/`):

- connects as the characters' controller and subscribes to `body_slot`, `body_brief`, `body_session`, `skill_card` and `law_override` (for `body_lease_ms`) only;
- per delegated character (`LIVING_BODY_ONLY` narrows them) opens a session: a new connection with no token (a fresh identity), `body_open`, then the player subscriptions of the world's delivery laws (`my_body`, `my_fighter`, `my_readiness`, `my_skill_uses`, `my_combat`, `combat_feed`, `seen_character_copy`, `seen_body_copy`, `seen_fighter_copy`, the cue tables, and the views for worlds with those laws off);
- each decision (15 Hz, `LIVING_BODY_HZ`) builds an `Observation` from those rows only, encodes it (`encode.rs`: the character, the target, 3 nearest others, 4 recent cues and the brief's typed values in a fixed vector of 99 floats (the brief's 8 traits included); no ids or names, no one's future release time, nothing about a creature not delivered), runs the policy and sends its intents through the player reducers (movement and the look only on a change); it beats every third of the lease;
- the policy is pluggable (`policy::Policy`) and chosen **per role**, the character's kind (`body_slot.role`: people, wolves), one instance shared by every character of that role (`policy::for_role`); none per individual;
- each character has its own small **memory** the worker carries between decisions (`policy::Memory`: a recurrent state of 16 values the policy may write and the last 4 intents it decided, 8 values each), reset on a new controller epoch and on a new brief revision; the policy reads and updates it, and nothing of one character's memory reaches another's decision;
- the pilot ships the scripted `HoldDistance` (hold a distance from the target, seeking it where it was last seen for 4 s once it leaves sight, its memory holding that place and its last press; under `engage`, press the first ready skill in scope within reach), not a trained net;
- on a stall past the lease, a lapsed session or an identity that no longer holds it, it drops what it decided, closes the old connection (its identity is fenced: no session holds it) and opens a new epoch with a new identity from a fresh observation. A timed-out press is never sent again.

**Capture** (`LIVING_BODY_CAPTURE=<dir>`, `capture.rs`): JSON lines, format version 1, in files rotated at `LIVING_BODY_CAPTURE_FILE_MB` (16) with at most `LIVING_BODY_CAPTURE_FILES` (8) kept. Records: `layout` (the flat vector's parts and sizes), `decision` (actor, role, epoch, sequence, the policy and its version, `flat`: the encoded vector of 99 then the memory the policy read, 48; the memory after, a summary of the delivered rows and their age, the slot, the brief with its traits, the intent encoded and as sent), `memory` (a reset), `reply` (each reducer's answer by sequence: accepted or the refusal's words), `outcome` (each skill use's resolved outcome from `my_skill_uses`) and `session` (opened, stalled, lost, closed). Joining decisions to outcomes is by actor, skill and time; nothing per inference is written to the database.

**Leakage tests** (before anything is trained): `body::encode::tests` (another creature's release time never reaches the vector; a creature not delivered contributes nothing, as an empty target slot; feed rows of another viewer are dropped; consistent renaming of every id changes nothing; an expired brief is none), and the verify-minds `delegate-body` scene's client checks (a session identity reads its own character's `my_body` and none of `body_brief`, `body_slot`, `body_session`, `brain`, `heed_policy`), with verify-client's privacy and permission matrix for the new tables and reducers.

**SpacetimeDB.** Docs read 2026-11-26 (labelled 2.0.0; the module pins 2.14.1): [tables](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/tables/) and [access permissions](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/tables/access-permissions/) (private: reducers and the owner only), [indexes](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/tables/indexes/) (a primary key with a separate unique `Identity` column; btree range filters), [reducers](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/functions/reducers/) and [error handling](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/functions/reducers/error-handling/) (an `Err` rolls back every write, so a refusal is the reply and the service journals it), [lifecycle](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/functions/reducers/lifecycle) (`client_disconnected` per connection), [views](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/functions/views/) (per-subscriber computation; the session's views are the player's own), [subscriptions](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/clients/subscriptions/), [the Rust client](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/clients/rust) (no token: a new identity and token), [automatic migrations](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/databases/automatic-migrations) (new tables keep clients connected; appended columns are avoided). Data paths: every read is a primary-key or unique lookup, or a btree range of due leases; the slot is written on change only; one session per delegated character, renewed by a beat every lease/3 or an accepted input; the views a session subscribes to are computed per subscriber, like a player's. Module memory is not used for any of it. Not measured: the cost of sessions at scale (the pilot runs one or two).

**Design input** (2026-11-26, from a study of Suárez's Neural MMO and PufferLib work relayed by the general coordinator; `~/slop-mq/notes/neural-body-suarez-input.md`). What it changed here: the policy interface carries a small recurrent state and a short intent history per character, reset on a new epoch or brief revision (a feedforward net forgets a target that leaves its local view); policies are selected per role and shared within it; individuality and identity travel in the brief (`traits`, stance, risk, style), never in per-character weights, which is also the honest answer to §4's per-life learning: one life's samples are far too few for its own weights, so a character's difference is its conditioning; the capture holds the flat input (observation and memory), the brief, the intent and the authority's outcome. ## 1. Where it fits

| Today | Proposed |
| --- | --- |
| The mind deliberates, writes graphs and routines, takes deliberate acts and consolidates experience. | Keep those jobs. Let the mind also install a short, readable brief for a learned executor. |
| Behaviour graphs run movement, combat and ordinary life on the authority. Combat decisions run around 15 Hz. | Give one executor control of the opted-in melee movement and skill inputs. The graph retains ordinary life and describes the plan. For a node assigned to the learned executor, it delegates that node's inputs for as long as it is active. The trial leaves distant navigation and social acts with the existing paths. |
| The fast lane can rewrite or switch a routine in response to a threat. It is off by default. | Keep it off in play. A prepared policy can use a fresh cue every decision without waiting for a patch. |
| The authority resolves movement, collision, checks, timing, costs, damage and death. Bevy presents what happens and collects player input. | Keep the authority and the same player input admission, budgets, checks, skill scripts and outcomes for both controllers. The net emits direction, facing, and ordinary skill press or release intents. |

R2's heavy fixture exposed an execution problem. Some early dodges had already ended at release. In the historical fast-lane arm, three of five subjects answered by exposure three, with two never answering. In R3, four of five veterans facing a new opponent had an answer prepared, against zero fresh subjects. Veterans answered 28/37 exposures versus 4/15, but median first answer was exposure two and median damage was 18 in both groups. Fresh fighters also drove the fixture off more often. Preparation helped evasion, with an unresolved trade between evasion and engagement. In the 100-fighter fast-lane trial the early frame cut contact from 95% to 46%. The balanced frame brought it back to 76%, mostly by keeping the existing plan. The lane stays off. **Body service.** Use the proposed NPC client service as transport and controller routing. The learned policy runs in a worker of `living/mind`, outside the authority thread. The body service hands it player-equivalent perceptions and batches its typed intents back to the shared player input path. The batch capability binds each entry to an assigned actor and controller epoch. Structural errors reject the envelope, and a legal envelope reports ordinary per-actor refusals independently. Each entry spends its own actor's input budget. Maintain one writer for movement and combat at a time, including while deliberate acts or an existing windup are in progress. The authority settles in-flight actions under the normal rules through ownership changes.

Use the same authorized per-character table projections a player client gets, including its own status and readiness, visible actors and combat cues, and own action outcomes (for example, `my_body`, `my_readiness`, `seen_character_copy`, `combat_seen` and `my_combat`). Consume only what was delivered by that decision tick. Encode nearby entities in bounded slots, relative positions, unknown and stale flags, and a short history of visible cues. A target leaving sight loses its live features. The policy may retain what it saw, with age and uncertainty. Avoid raw identity features, fixture labels, future release timestamps from internal schedules, invisible actors, and observer-wide tables. If the player feed lacks a cue that the presentation legitimately conveys, define the shared cue first or test with the cues already available. Perception routing and the model each get a leakage test. A batched tensor groups separate character rows and has no cross-character attention or shared runtime state.

The NPC service prototype measured about 330 → 267 ms of DB-thread work per second when it drove 200 fighters, before charging for full NPC perception. Its estimates with player-grade perception are about 470–550 ms/s on the DB thread or about 307 with the proposed perception helper. The relay and helper are therefore dependencies for a scaled deployment. Measure the actual sets, not just the synthetic input savings. ## 2. What the mind can write and read

Follow the implemented `heed_policy` / `mind_heed` pattern. Propose a private record keyed by actor, with one active revision, a bounded expiry and a typed reducer for replace or clear. “Rewired” means changing these inputs to a fixed, versioned net. An example brief in the mind's own vocabulary is “Keep the spear's distance from the person I can see. Stay engaged while I have breath to defend. Prefer measured attacks.” Its fields could be a goal and optional remembered place, an optional scoped target, stance, risk and style scalars. The policy learns how to realize them. A brief authorizes no new action and binds no skill timing or reaction to a cue. Preserve the mind's own rationale as text for its next deliberation. The net receives typed values only. [Attention budget](ATTENTION_BUDGET.md).

Check the caller's controller assignment, living actor, expected revision, known goal and style schema, finite bounded values, expiry and target eligibility. Give the same refusal for unseen and nonexistent targets. Return a truthful refusal through the reducer response and keep the earlier accepted record. A failed transaction cannot also durably write its own refusal, so the service journals the actual response. Expose the current accepted brief, remaining time, provenance and active plan node to the owning mind through its scoped interface. Show what the controller used by revision and how stale its senses were. A replacement takes effect prospectively, and conditioning on remembered facts respects the character's own memory.

**Database surface.** The authority pins SpacetimeDB 2.14.1. The checked-in heed path and player bindings are version-pinned evidence for private rows, controller-checked writes and scoped delivery. The public docs are labelled 2.0.0. No live service inspection was needed for this design. Use indexed actor-key lookups for current briefs, leases and input admission. Write briefs on revision or removal at death, update leases by batch with bounded expiry work, and reuse incremental player projections and existing skill outcomes. The owner sees its own accepted data. Audit readers use separately authorized records. Measure fanout and per-recipient view invalidations before adding any hot view. Use event delivery for transient cues where appropriate, with durable sources for recovery and learning. Keep sampled perceptions and full trial traces in bounded external capture rather than adding DB rows per inference. ## 3. How the mind reads its body

| Option | What the mind can read | Perception and truth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Report habits | The accepted brief and recent choices as counts, ranges and examples, such as three earlier dodges at this spacing. This describes what has happened under the brief. | Summaries use only the character's delivered perceptions and its own actions and outcomes. A small sample is labelled as such. Weights and activations supply no facts about the world. |
| (b) Explain choices | A short account in the mind's terms linking the brief, visible cue, attempt and outcome. This can make a surprising action easier to inspect. | Generate it from the recorded brief and trace, with links to the evidence. An explanation of why the model chose an action is a hypothesis unless separately established. A cue preceding an action proves their ordering. |
| (c) Keep a legible plan | Keep the mind-written graph as the readable plan. An opted-in leaf declares its target, goal, entry and exit conditions and skill scope. The learned executor chooses local movement and skill timing within that assignment. | The delegated slot uses player-equivalent perceptions and ordinary inputs. Graph conditions controlling that slot must also use the character's permitted facts. The active leaf describes intent and scope, while actual actions and outcomes come from the trace. |

**Use (c) for the pilot.** Before acting, the mind reads the same plan it wrote, what it delegated and the scope of the delegation, so it can tell what the body is meant to do. It cannot predict the exact dodge or footstep. After acting it reads a compact factual trace. A leaf such as “hold spear distance and stay engaged until I lose sight” leaves open when to move or use a skill. It should never contain a cue-to-action recipe for the net to follow.

The mind reads its current brief alongside the same kind of evidence it reads with graphs today. Summarize recent attempted moves, refused inputs, and resolved results with links to their source events. For example, “You started a dodge before the swing, and the blow hit when it landed” is supported by own action and outcome times. “You dodged too early” is an inference the mind can make if the evidence supports it. Missing outcomes stay unknown. A policy score, activation or generated explanation never stands in for evidence. Consolidation can use this record between encounters.

## 4. How it learns

1. **Clone actions from play.** Record consenting human players' inputs and existing graph rollouts together with the exact player-equivalent packets available before those inputs. Keep refusals and no-op intervals. Label graph actions only when they can be expressed through the player interface, and keep their source and brief. A graph can read a fact unavailable to the player path, so audit the teacher and exclude those examples. Split by encounter, session and opponent configuration before fitting. Human timing and different viable responses are valuable. Graph data are a cheap starting point and can also reproduce the graph's mistakes.
2. **Improve in the real authority.** If the clone can complete a duel, gather new episodes through the same input path in headless scratch worlds using the module's `det_clock` stepping. Freeze the build, laws and skill hashes. A training driver needs an observation barrier and tick-stamped input admission through the same checks. Today's comparator stops after a fixed tick count, and external reducers can read wall time. Add deterministic input staging on the scratch diagnostic path and prove exact same-input replay before interpreting fine-tuning results. Use the authority's actual outcomes for reward and evaluation. A small actor-critic fine-tune with an imitation penalty is one option after the first experiment. Use fresh starts and randomized legal schedules, and hold back variants and player traces. [Authority clock source](../living/authority/src/det.rs), [Deterministic comparator and clock setup](../living/tools/det_compare.py), [Real-authority combat comparisons](COMBAT.md).
3. **Learn within one life.** Ship a fixed generic base trained on offline play and scratch arenas. Start each new character with that motor competence and empty personal memory. Keep up to 128 salient, verified examples from its own observations and outcomes. The mind can see the evidence, age and confidence, and the body can receive a compact retrieval summary. If that improves performance, test a rank-4 adapter over the 96 → 64 layer (about 640 weights before metadata, about 2.6 KB FP32 per life). Collect an illustrative 2k–10k decisions per life, about 2–11 active minutes at 15 Hz before filtering. Require multiple encounters with useful coverage and retain whole encounters for within-life validation. A rare opponent can take much longer to meet. At 10 passes, backpropagating through a 20k-weight policy puts an update on the order of 2.4–12 GFLOP per eligible life, before validation and optimizer overhead. At 200 eligible lives the update work is separate for each life, even when scheduled in batches. Warm-start from the base, fit off the tick, and publish a new version atomically only if it helps on that life’s held-out evidence. Keep the base and summary when the sample is thin. A pooled learner could learn more quickly from rare encounters, but pooling live updates would circulate knowledge that characters have never acquired. Expect noisier adaptation and weaker early quality on uncommon patterns. Key the adapter and its experience to world and life, keep both on our machines, and clear active state at death. Knowledge can still pass through ordinary teaching or communication.

Score broad outcomes appropriate to the current goal. Survival alone buys disengagement, damage alone buys trades or exploits, time in range buys standing still, and a reward for pressing dodge on a windup gives the tactic away. For an engage brief, examine resolution, damage and resource use together with contact and return after separation. For a withdraw brief, leaving can be success. Exclude fixture-internal state from the policy, avoid per-cue action labels in the reward, and test varied timing, identities and starts. Inspect failures, including early evasion, input spam, boundary abuse and perfect clock anticipation. Condition swaps and history ablation test whether decisions track the goal and what the character saw. A fixed net reacting to a cue demonstrates control. A changed response following experiences from the same life is separate evidence of learning.

## 5. Timing, load and replay

The authority still ticks every 16.67 ms. A 15 Hz controller samples every four ticks, 10 Hz every six. Sampling alone can add up to 67 or 100 ms. Long-lived steering uses the existing player input semantics; discrete presses have deadlines. Start at 15 Hz for duels. Phase agents across ticks, cap queue delay, send partial batches on deadline and coalesce stale steering. The proposed local budget from delivered observation to submitted input is 5 ms p95, including up to 2 ms waiting to batch and a 2 ms compute target. Measure publication and delivery age, sample wait, submit-to-admit, and admit-to-effect independently. The NPC study's relay measured 21 ms median and 34–35 ms p99 tick-to-client age in its gate setup. A cue can already be old when inference begins. Report misses against the actual release window and the unchanged 50 ms p95 / 100 ms p99 server receipt-to-commit contract, excluding declared cast times. [Performance contract](PERFORMANCE_CONTRACT.md), [NPC service measurements](NPC_CLIENT_SERVICE.md).

## 8. Risks and recommendation

A controller can overfit arena timing, exploit training rewards, pick a style the mind did not ask for, learn from hidden features, or become brittle when its observations arrive late. A body-service outage leaves assigned NPCs idle, and a reconnect can otherwise admit old input. Resolve these with changed schedules, paired brief tests, scoped trace provenance, a single controller epoch and stall/reconnect checks. The duel can establish whether the learned layer improves control. Group coordination, human-facing feel, the 216-character 30-minute gate and the 2,000-character eight-hour target each need their own evidence.

**Pursue the narrower variant.** Run one CPU-first, conditioned melee controller under a legible graph leaf, with recorded player-equivalent inputs and real-authority evaluation. R2 gives a concrete timing failure and R3 gives a reason to keep the mind's preparation in the loop. Require a gain over the matched graph without loss of engagement before investing in reinforcement, personal adapters or a scaled body service.

### Decision (throughput gate)
