The native implementation and its immutable freeze are still future gates. Preparing this plan and its [campaign configuration](../configs/experiments/campaign/028-faction-reality-admission.json) launches no host, worker, database or model request. Candidate paths refer to the forthcoming immutable `reality-m7-10` freeze; they do not claim that the bundle or qualifying fixture already exists.

The question is whether native admission for eight external `tools/call` RPCs can provide successful scoped reads and ordinary late model opportunities in the existing 36-person world, while retained connections and stopped-host finalization complete within the original bounds. [Campaign 026](SOCIETY_BATCH_026.md) remains failed: it lost repeated external setup access and exceeded cleanup supervision. Its final snapshot and later all-39 cleanup retain their original labels.

`recovery: true` retains 026's ordinary controller feedback behavior. It does not resume the failed world or authorize campaign retries. The scenario must preserve every person, body, stock, location, affiliation, private record, survival policy and territorial grant. In particular, actors 2, 6, 10 and 14 retain their own local grants and external route. No law source, cases, successful proof, transferred discovery, stronger model or required action is added.

The new native admission gate uses cross-process file locks shared by this run's external clients. It applies only to `tools/call`, including scoped `observe` and mutation calls. `server/discover` and `tools/list` do not acquire these permits. Each admitted RPC holds one of eight permits through its response/error/cancellation boundary and releases it afterward. Provider calls execute outside the permit: models remain asynchronous, with no new model-concurrency cap. Built-in requests, owner exports and the controlled browser observer remain outside this external gate. Eight permits therefore describe admitted external RPCs, not a service-wide authority work limit; unknown late authority delivery remains possible after a client timeout.

The **existing 15-second whole-RPC deadline starts before admission**, and permit wait, write, flush and response share that one budget. The 5-second participant readiness and 10-second atomic receipt limits are unchanged and remain bounded by the outer RPC deadline. Admission waiting can exhaust the budget before dispatch; that remains a failed opportunity. No deadline extension, provider retry, command replay, transport reconnect, worker respawn or automatic experiment repeat is added. File-lock cleanup must release permits on normal completion, errors, cancellation and process death without deleting or replacing live lock identities. Record actual admission wait/acquisition, RPC dispatch/flush/completion, permit release and outcome evidence, including a call that never acquired a permit. These records must separate queue delay from downstream delay without inventing authority commit times.

Legacy `per_call` starts its 15-second response timer after flush. The prior persistent m7-9 condition used one 15-second budget for write/flush/response; m7-10 adds admission to that same budget. This is a declared load-admission change, not extra time for the previously failed reads. The new fixture's frozen criteria must match the actual implementation. Fairness, throughput, and success with eight permits are measurements to establish, not assumptions.

The production host, owned workers and their reader/child processes are stopped before final capture. Finalization then pauses the exact run, enumerates and revokes all of its grants, including observer grants, checks zero grants and a paused clock, and captures one World with its exact audit cutoff. Cleanup failures remain failures even if later best-effort revocation succeeds. Other campaigns retain their existing defaults; this campaign explicitly requests all three settings: persistent transport, eight RPC permits and stopped-host finalization.

## Predeclared model-run acceptance

The acceptance target is this bounded engineering integration, not autonomous law invention. A finite pass requires all of the following evidence:

- **Measured duration and original cleanup deadline.** Preserve the actual active-start/end timestamps and 720-second requested duration, excluding cleanup. Drain/cancel worker jobs, stop owned workers and host, pause/revoke/verify, write the coherent final export and finish the pilot before the existing batch supervision deadline of gate release plus 720 + 60 seconds. Also report active-end-to-pilot-finish duration; it must be at most 60 seconds. Individual 30-second CLI limits are not proof of this aggregate bound. Do not extend the batch allowance or reinterpret an interrupted pilot as complete.
- **Initial and later access.** Retain each person's initial actual scoped model context, not only grant rows or a successful provider status. For external actors, audit every scheduled setup/discovery/list/admission/observation outcome until death or the active boundary. A missing journal must have an explicit setup, timeout, cancellation or transport explanation; no silent replacement or retry qualifies. In-flight work canceled at the active boundary is recorded separately from an earlier unavailable connection.
- **Late opportunities.** For every external actor alive throughout each wall interval `[240,480)` and `[480,720)` seconds after active start, require at least one successful actor-scoped observation followed by a genuine model request dispatched in that interval. Preserve the worker/RPC times and corresponding context, whose simulation time or observation cursor advances from earlier requests, and reconcile the authority read. A surviving actor with no qualifying opportunity fails this finite access criterion even if one slow model job was still in flight; that outcome is reported as insufficient coverage, not automatically as a transport defect. Actors dying within an interval retain all attempts and exact death evidence, without inventing later opportunities. Report all four editors individually, including early death or loss of access.
- **Outcome separation.** HTTP success, a parseable proposal, an intentional no-operation, native schema failure, an authority rejection, an accepted command and its later physical execution are different results. A valid no-operation or an invalid model proposal can still establish that a real model opportunity occurred. An unavailable setup cannot. Match timeout request IDs to retained authority events where possible; keep ambiguous mappings and late commits explicit.
- **Coherent original final evidence.** Require pilot and batch completion without interruption or cleanup errors. Preserve final World identity/hash and exactly contiguous same-run events `1..next_event-1`, all grant-revocation outcomes, the paused clock, stopped owned processes and original final timestamps. Check every prior owned database remains paused or empty with zero grants. Any recovery is separately named, measured and hashed and cannot replace the original result.
- **Resource and invariant results.** One-second process/host/cgroup samples must cover publication through cleanup with continuous identities, no dropped guard samples, no extra swap, no new limit/OOM failure and host `MemAvailable` at least 4 GiB. Record any resource abort as trial failure. Retain the original disk reserve and map only this new database's physical replica for WAL; distinguish logical growth, net allocation, sampled positive growth and window endpoints. Run the existing food, electricity/water/parts, knowledge-copy, research, population, scope and law audits on eligible original final evidence. Report engine faults and all legitimate current-law authorization denials separately.

Assign model opportunities using measured request-dispatch wall timestamps when available, or a conservatively enclosing interval from actual retained markers. Do not invent an exact timestamp from job start, file mtime or RPC elapsed duration. An interval qualifies only when the whole certified bound lies within the late window, the scoped read is reconciled, and its context advances in simulation time or observation cursor from an earlier observation. Those subjective values establish freshness, not wall dispatch time.

Establish survival from original-final living state under permanent death, or authority death events ordered against uniquely matched successful atomic reads with actual wall bounds. Reads by another external actor in the same run may bracket a death: death before a read whose job finished before the window end proves the actor was not alive throughout it; a read preceding death whose RPC started after the window end proves prior survival. Provider failure after a successful read does not erase that death-timing evidence. Preserve exact witnesses and ambiguous intervals. Already-retained fresh authority checkpoints may also be used; do not add full-World exports solely to classify deaths. Insufficient bounds stay uncertified.

The two late intervals are a prospective sampling criterion, not proof of continuous availability at every instant. Report starts, successful observations, model requests, native results and deaths per actor and time interval, including failed attempts. Do not count paused-cleanup journals as active opportunities. If all actors die before twelve minutes, preserve the terminal result under the existing supervisor contract, but it cannot satisfy this plan's full twelve-minute late-access coverage target.
