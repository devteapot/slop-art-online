# Reply earshot measurement

Four of 33 submitted, directed mind talk replies missed their intended listener across play-tests 8, 9 and the play-test 10 launch (12.1%). The three misses with position samples were inside ordinary speech range around model completion and outside it at submission. The launch evidence identifies Agnes as the mover and leaves her distance at model completion unresolved.

## Where the wait happens

On the examined main revision `5fd86af50e81b94749b19a7dc6a5a340a19485a9`, [`reply_turn`](../living/mind/src/mind/talk.rs#L1065) awaits the provider and parses the reply before computing the [wait](../living/mind/src/mind/talk.rs#L1178). It sleeps for the positive remainder until the later of the replier's last recorded line plus 2,100 ms and the incoming line's authority time plus [`saying_ms(heard.text)`](../living/mind/src/mind/talk.rs#L1525). The latter is 330 ms per whitespace-delimited word bounded to 1,500–8,000 ms. It represents time for the other person to finish speaking. The other deadline is a mind-side rate guard. The [speech submission](../living/mind/src/mind/talk.rs#L1259) also retries after 2,100 ms on a “too fast” error. Authority enforces its own [2,000 ms speaker gap and way checks](../living/authority/src/speech.rs#L46).

Fern's six-word **incoming greeting** gives a 1,980 ms deadline measured from receipt. The journal and action clocks put roughly 674 ms before model completion and about 1,306 ms between completion and submission. The 1,980 ms is the total read-aloud budget, including the time that inference already used.

The [speech reducer path](../living/authority/src/speech.rs#L82) samples positions at the reducer timestamp and computes each listener's reach with `way.carry_for(listener hearing)`. The listener set is evaluated after the sleep. Facing and sight affect [the silence wording](../living/authority/src/speech.rs#L330). The truth is recorded as a speaker-side experience. The mind's [silence handler](../living/mind/src/mind/talk.rs#L579) sets the pair's `ended` flag and returns. The result already exists in experience and can appear under “Lately” in a later prompt. There is no immediate decision about the failed delivery.

## Retrospective estimates from the real-session baseline

- **(d) Submit when ready and pace presentation.** Three of the four cases are strong recovery candidates under the observed paths and original replies. The fourth depends on when Agnes crossed the boundary. Conditional retrospective estimate **3–4 of 4**, with no live intervention result yet. Preserve the authority's rate and way checks. Pace the display without re-evaluating recipients at display time.
- **(a) Give the model distance and its own motion.** All four missed frames lacked those current facts. With (d), the added information could recover **0–1 additional baseline misses**. The remaining case would still require usable perceived information and the model choosing an effective response. Independent recovery from (a) alone is unmeasured. Use perceived current distance where available and label a last-heard distance with its age. The authority's indexed local state and scoped perception remain the source of facts. Keep row replication bounded to the existing controller's scope. [SpacetimeDB subscriptions](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/clients/subscriptions/) and [sender-aware views](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/functions/views/).
- **(b) Acts chosen with speech.** The [bystanders-2 interface](BYSTANDERS_2.md) already accepts `goto` alongside a line. It approaches a target and does not express a durable stay. `face` is a contextual turn skill with visibility and reach checks. Its standing turn, on its own, changes no acoustic distance. A rooted skill may stop motion during performance. The current talk path submits speech before running acts, so a same-turn act recovers **0 of 4 original deliveries** in this ordering. An explicit approach could help a later attempt.
- **(c) Give the mind a chance to respond to failed delivery.** Truthful feedback could plausibly recover **2 of 4** via a new call: the next sampled separation after the speaker gap was still below 30 m for Baldwin–Petra and Aldric–Maud. Fern is an additional plausible case with a stationary listener and no distance series. Petra was about 33 m from Agnes one second after the miss and 37 m after roughly 2.4 seconds, so a stationary retry after the 2 s floor likely fails. An approach may work if the speaker chooses to pursue and can reacquire the addressee. Estimated plausible call opportunities **2–3 of 4**, with no observed recovery. This is worth considering as a follow-up for residual misses, with one opportunity to choose what to do and no forced repeat. Its incremental benefit on this baseline after (d) is at most one case by the estimate above.

The recommendations above were based on the historical evidence. The paired replay measures the landed changes to timing and follow-up. It did not drive a rendered client or measure the cadence of successive replies from one replier.
