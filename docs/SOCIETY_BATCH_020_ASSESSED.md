## Original assessment now unlocks authoring

The standard final audit identifies six personal built-in forecast proofs: invention Ari, Bryn and Cato; transfer-repeat Bryn; loss-risk Ari and Cato. Cooling has none. These proofs belong to the operator who paid for, retrieved and assessed the report. Copying a report does not transfer that personal experience.

Loss-risk Ari's exact first chain is forecast submission 1444, completion 1616, retrieval 5078 and private report source 5079. His learning observation at 140.724 seconds actually includes that source in its leased evidence page. The accepted reflection creates a derived assertion and original-report interpretation 6769 at 149.619 seconds. His next behavior observation at 164.852 seconds contains the interpreted original holding, the personal proof and `can_author:true`. He then chooses to archive the derived assertion rather than prototype. This directly demonstrates that the repaired assessment operation works even with a non-null knowledge draft.

Transfer Bryn independently cites a fresh reread source 18040 in his learning observation at 302.315 seconds. Accepted interpretation 20438 at 318.221 seconds marks the original report. His next behavior observation at 334.294 seconds receives `can_author:true` and returns no operation. Both actors' first unlocked contexts expose a usable local terminal, sufficient electricity and water, and an empty queue. Missing authoring permission or a missing prototype affordance does not explain those particular choices.

Invention Ari's behavior call, observed at 960.549 seconds, eventually proposes a prototype-shaped policy accepted as 47200. Its source is `pub fn technique(input) { input }`, with input `[7]` and prediction `[7]`. Execution rejects the source with `Undefined variable: pub` 243 times between 1007.113 and 1080.272 seconds; no numeric job is created and no prototype quantum is paid. His later learning observation at 1043.767 seconds addresses the syntax failures, and reflection 50732 is accepted. The final behavior call begins removing the retry loop but is cancelled at cutoff. The attempted program is an identity function, not a solution to the seeded multi-interval storage problem. Even a successful identity test would not establish useful nonlinear invention or holdout behavior.

The initial forecast interface also had a documented ambiguity: the first actual invention request exposes personally held record IDs, but its `sources` schema is merely an array of strings and does not explain the required record-ID namespace. Ari initially supplies descriptive phrases and receives repeated validation failures. Later actual requests contain the exact held IDs and explicit source-ID error feedback; he removes the failing research loop. This supports a concrete initial documentation gap, without proving that this gap caused every later decision or the absence of useful invention. The trial was not repaired in place. The supplied interface also says “Public fn technique(input)” without an exact minimal declaration; the executable fixtures use `fn technique(input) { ... }` with public visibility implicit. That syntax ambiguity is separate from source-ID documentation and does not establish why the actor chose `pub`.
