# Checkpoint 3: a larger world of towns and settlers

Direction from the user (2026-09-26), built after [checkpoint 2](CHECKPOINT_2.md). Plan below; progress and evidence at the end.

## Goals

1. **A much bigger world.** A map several times larger (on the order of 256×256 tiles: 256 chunks), with distinct regions — coast, rivers, forests, hills, plains — so distance, travel and territory matter. The authority's chunk index, kinematic motion and novelty-driven experiences are designed for this; the benchmark confirms it before minds are added.
2. **Established towns on one side.** Two or three towns with a real footprint (homes, storages, fields of planted bushes, workshops, a meeting place), stocked stores and dozens of inhabitants each, who have lived there a while: relationships, know-how spread unevenly, roles already practiced. They need to leave town to gather, hunt, fish and cut wood, so roles and cooperation matter and trade between towns has reasons.
3. **Settlers from almost nothing on the other side.** Small bands with little know-how and no structures, in the wilds, like the current valley. The question is whether they build up toward what the towns have — by discovery, by contact and trade with towns, or not at all.
4. **Trade, conflict and roles as emergent behavior**, supported by mechanics rather than scripts:
   - Exchange: an offer/accept primitive so a trade is atomic and trust-bearing (both give, or neither), on top of existing `give`.
   - Communities as world state: a named group with members, a home area and shared storage permissions that members found, join, leave or expel (institutions are part of the world vision); taking from a storage one is not entitled to is visible theft.
   - No assigned jobs: roles show up in what people repeatedly do and in their own identities.
5. **Real-time combat that feels like two players.** The performance contract's 60 Hz combat with windups, dodges, blocks and projectiles:
   - Combat skills with timing: attack windup/recovery, dodge (a short dash with a vulnerable/invulnerable window), block, thrown spear; stamina.
   - Perception of intent: seeing an opponent wind up is observable, so graphs can react (`{"sees_attack": ...}` → dodge or block).
   - Engaged characters are evaluated at combat cadence (10–20 Hz) instead of 1 Hz; the tick budget covers a 200-character local battle.
   - Tactics learned and patched mid-fight: after hits, the mind patches the labeled combat subtree (not the whole graph) with what it has learned about this opponent; consolidation keeps lessons for later fights.
6. **Skills, discovery, creation, knowledge sharing in depth.** Beyond the fixed ladder: characters with mastery compose new named techniques from existing script primitives (roadmap Stage 6), share them by teaching and tablets, and see them validated by the same rules.
7. **Viewer**: a large-map experience — minimap, smooth zoom levels, community/territory overlays, combat readability (windups, hits, projectiles), follow camera and a timeline of notable events.

## Evidence to look for

Towns persist and provision themselves through members' work outside; settlers survive and grow (structures, know-how, population) or fail understandably; contact between towns and settlers produces trade, teaching, migration or conflict with interpretable causes; a 1v1 and a group fight show reactive dodging/blocking and tactic changes between engagements; the 2,000-character and 200-battle benchmarks stay within the performance contract; LLM calls per minute stay bounded as population grows.

## Monitoring

While it runs: a status/evidence snapshot about every 15 minutes (population and causes of death, know-how spread, trades and thefts, community membership, combat outcomes, LLM rate and failures), with unusual behavior investigated and fixed at its cause.

## Implementation plan

1. **Map size as data.** `world` carries width/height; chunk ids become width-independent (`cy << 16 | cx`); `Map` carries its dimensions; the viewer reads them from the `world` row and renders terrain in chunks (a single 4096² texture does not scale).
2. **Realm generator** ([realm.rs](../living/rules/src/realm.rs), done): elevation sloping west→east to a sea coast, mountains and hills in the west, rivers carved downhill to the sea, forests and plains by moisture, fords cut so all land connects, and proposed town and wild sites.
3. **Realm seed.** Towns (name, site, population, occupations as history rather than assignment, structure layout — shelters as homes, storages, fires, planted fields, a meeting sign with town records, a community per town, relationships and uneven know-how) and wild bands (3–5 people each, little know-how, no structures); wildlife scaled to the land. Town residents' personas are generated by the mind at first start from their town, history and relatives, then kept as ordinary identity.
4. **LLM budget and priority.** A global rate limit with priority (being attacked, being spoken to > dawn and plan failures > consolidation), per-species pacing (already in `species.json`), and measurement of calls, tokens and latency per character type. Measured on valley-5 (12 people, 19 animals): 41.6 calls/min and ~220k tokens/min before consolidation pacing; animals dominated because of alarm-call noise, since fixed.
5. **Benchmarks** on the realm before minds: 2,000 characters across 256×256 and a 200-character battle, within the performance contract.
6. **Viewer**: chunked terrain, a realm-scale minimap, a community panel (members, stores, standing), and follow/jump-to-event controls.

## Progress (2026-09-26)

Implemented: plan items 1–6 in their first form — map size as data, the realm generator, the realm seed ([realm.json](../living/seeds/realm.json): Oakhollow inland and Saltmere on the coast with 14 residents each, three wild bands of 3–4, 36 deer, 6 wolves), background-generated identities, the LLM budget with priority, realm benchmarks (see [living core](LIVING_CORE.md#measured-authority-load)) and the chunked viewer with minimap and community panel. Mechanism checks pass on the realm (7/7). The live world `realm-1` started 2026-09-26 02:17 UTC.

Problems found by watching realm-1 and fixed at their cause:

- Remembered places were clamped to the old 96-tile valley, so townspeople walked to (96, 96) instead of home; 733 corrupted place coordinates were cleared in the minds and homes, town centers and camps restored from world state.
- Minds called everything "valley": the authority's novelty experience and birth text said so; now neutral.
- Graphs were rejected for filtering people by the relationship labels minds use (`partner`, `ally`); any label is now matched against the relationship's own label.
- Nearly everyone sat at energy 4 by day 3: an `if` dropped a running action as soon as its condition stopped holding, so the sleep reflex woke people at 4.1. Work that completes on its own (sleep, eat, gather, build, …) is now finished once begun under an `if`; movement and combat stay reactive. The reflex layer became bodily habits (eat carried food above 65 hunger; sleep below 12 energy, or when tired at night beside a fire or shelter; when starving take from a storage, gather, or head home). `refresh_reflexes` re-wraps every mind's current graph after such a change. By day 5 most people's energy was back at 70–100.
- 27 deer and all 6 wolves starved: the small animal minds reason themselves into fear ("stay still… do not eat, too weak to flee") and the compiled graphs never reach grazing. Eating when starving is a species body drive now (deer graze above 70 hunger unless a wolf is near; hungry wolves attack deer in sight).
- Saltmere ate through its seeded stores by day 5 without restocking them, while a few people carry 50–117 berries; food exists (the town's berry ring regrows about twice what the town eats), so provisioning is left to the people.

**Combat** ([duel.py](../living/tools/duel.py): two people with a feud, each with its own mind, on a separate database; nothing forces a fight). First run: the attacked person fled and was cut down from behind, because a telegraphed blow landed however far the target had run. Blows now miss when the target is out of reach at the end of the windup (plus a species lunge from the script), fighters get a short account of the exchange every ~7 s, and the rules explain that reactions must come before the strike. Afterwards: one fight of 12 blows where the defender blocked 9 and the attacker patched only its `combat` branch mid-fight to throw its spear at a defender who kept blocking (“Your blocks won't save you forever”); one short exchange that broke off, with the defender vowing to meet the attacker at the ridge; one run where they never met. No dodges yet in mind-driven fights (the mechanism dodges in the scripted battle benchmark).
- Five people starved by winter (day 7), two of them in Saltmere beside a full berry bush. Sania stood on a bush with berries for hours of game time, "gathering", and never got one: an action that could not start (taking food from the now-empty store, the first option of the starving habit) cancelled the running gather before failing, every second. An action now has to be startable, including its rules check when it starts on the spot, before it abandons the current one. This affected any priority list whose higher option could not start.
- Mistral Small returned invalid JSON for 11% of deliberations (220 of 1,959; Mistral Medium 0.2%), some as runaway generations of up to 130k characters. Model calls now carry an output cap (4,000 tokens by default, per profile).

**Group fight** (`duel.py --per-side 3`, 6 min): 15 hits, 3 blows that found only air, one mid-fight `combat` patch, one death (Walker4, killed by Walker3). Fighters named targets and coordinated ("Walker2, Walker3—now! Flank him!"; Walker3 finished Walker4, then turned to Walker5 "while protecting Walker1 and Walker2"); one of the raiders never joined, gathering berries and greeting a passing stranger instead.

After the start-guard fix (day 8, winter, 30 min later): no further person starved; deer starvation fell to 3 and wolves made four kills; deer then died of exhaustion (14), since their fearful minds rarely rest, so deer and wolves got a sleep drive too. Speech began to turn from survival logistics to people: teaching writing "so our records don't fade", a first accusation of theft ("Minogar, your actions undermine us—stop taking without asking"), requests to trade hides, wanting to learn tracking.
