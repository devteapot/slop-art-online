# Authored plans: a settlement laid out by hand

Started 2026-11-11 (branch `plan-1`, Cullet phase 2's engine part; [the small city](SMALL_CITY.md#8-build-plan), decision 10). A land seed can now describe a settlement as written: its ground levels at exact heights, its streets, stairs, square, harbour and every lot with its model and height. The generator lays the rest of the land round it and steps aside for it. Nothing in the engine knows about Cullet: a plan is seed data, read by general code.

**Status.** Implemented and verified on scratch databases on the dev-box (SpacetimeDB 2.14.1 in its own containers, `plan1-stdb` and `plan2-stdb`). The test seed is [`living/seeds/plan-test.json`](../living/seeds/plan-test.json): a 9-lot harbour town on seed 3 at 1024 m. Since 2026-11-11 (branch `plan-2`) [`living/seeds/cullet.json`](../living/seeds/cullet.json) is the authored city: Cullet's 93 lots (65 buildings) on the Wrack, an island shaped by an [authored coastline and relief stamps](#authored-land-the-coast-and-stamps), written by [`living/tools/cullet_plan.py`](../living/tools/cullet_plan.py) ([below](#cullets-plan)). Seeds with no `plans`, no `relief.outline` and no `relief.stamps` produce byte-identical lands (`host::tests::pinned_digests_hold`).

## The format

A plan is an entry of `map.params.plans` (`living_rules::settle::PlanSpec`). Coordinates are metres in the plan's own frame: x east, y north (uphill, for Cullet), from `origin`. `origin` is a point on the land (land metres: x east, y south). `rotation_deg` turns the plan's north to that compass bearing on the land. The conversion uses `living_rules::det` trigonometry only, so the authority and every client derive the same land.

```json
"map": {"kind": "land", "size": 1024, "params": {
  "sites": [{"kind": "village", "min": 2, "max": 2, "per_km2": 0.0, "radius_m": 50.0, "spacing_m": 300.0, "scale": 1.0, "flatten": 0.75}],
  "plans": [{
    "id": "plan_test", "kind": "town", "style": "cullet",
    "origin": [575, 888], "rotation_deg": 15,
    "levels": [
      {"id": "quay", "height_m": 2.5, "area": [[-40, 0], [40, 0], [40, 24], [-40, 24]]},
      {"id": "row",  "height_m": 6.5, "area": [[-40, 24], [40, 24], [40, 54], [-40, 54]]}
    ],
    "sea": [[-60, -70], [60, -70], [60, 0], [-60, 0]],
    "square": {"level": "quay", "area": [[-12, 2], [12, 2], [12, 14], [-12, 14]], "paved": true},
    "streets": [
      {"id": "the row", "points": [[-38, 40], [38, 40]], "width_m": 4.0, "class": "street", "surface": "setts", "level": "row"},
      {"id": "the road out", "points": [[38, 40], [55, 62], [72, 96]], "width_m": 3.6, "class": "trunk"}
    ],
    "stairs": [{"id": "the steps", "from": "quay", "to": "row", "foot": [32, 18], "head": [32, 28], "width_m": 2.4, "rise_m": 4.0}],
    "lots": [
      {"id": "hall", "kind": "hall", "name": "the Tally Hall", "at": [0, 19], "size": [16, 9], "facing": "south", "level": "quay",
       "model": "cullet/hall/tally.glb", "height_m": 14.0, "landmark": true,
       "spots": [{"name": "under the arch", "at": [-4, 5.5]}]},
      {"id": "house_a", "kind": "house", "at": [-20, 32.5], "size": [6, 9], "facing": "north", "level": "row", "height_m": 8.5, "door_colour": "oxblood"}
    ]
  }]
}}
```

| Field | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | required | The site's seed id: `towns[].site` names it, as `town` or `steampunk_town` name generated sites. Unique across plans. |
| `kind`, `style` | `town`, required | The settlement's kind and `settlement.style`: the client's asset folder (`settle::env_of`; `cullet` is `envs/cullet/`). |
| `origin`, `rotation_deg` | required, 0 | The frame (above). |
| `scale` | 1 | Inhabitants' height relative to a 1.75 m adult. |
| `drop_m` | 0.6 | Width of the drop where a level meets a lower one. The ground falls inside the lower level, along its edge. |
| `skirt_m` | 10 | Width over which the plan's outer edge blends into the natural ground (never over water). |
| `blend_m` | 60 | How far round the levels the land's coarse relief is pulled toward them, so the hill meets the town gently. |
| `sea_depth_m` | 6 | Depth stamped inside `sea`. |
| `levels[]` | at least one, at most 64 | `id`, `height_m`, `area` (a polygon), `retaining` (default true: retaining walls stand along its edges where the ground falls more than a step below it). Levels may share edges; they may not overlap. |
| `sea` | none | A polygon forced to sea before the water is found (a harbour). It must reach the open sea to be sea, and may not overlap a level. |
| `square` | required | `level`, `area` (inside that level), `paved` (default true). The settlement's square: its centre is the settlement's centre and newcomers' arrival. |
| `streets[]` | none | `points`, `width_m`, `class` (`trunk`, `street`, `lane`, `path`; default `street`), `surface` (for clients; the rules do not read it), `level` (if given, every point must lie on it). Streets lie on the plan's ground as written: no grading, no shoulders. |
| `stairs[]` | none | `from` (lower level), `to` (higher level), `foot` (on `from`) and `head` (on `to`), `width_m` (2.4), `rise_m` (if given, it must equal the levels' difference within 5 cm), `model`. A flight rises at most 1 m per metre. |
| `lots[]` | none | `id` (unique), `kind` (a `LotKind`: `house`, `townhouse`, `inn`, `hall`, `chapel`, `workshop`, `barn`, `tower`, `well`, `market_stall`, `lamp`, `garden`, `field`), `name`, `at` (centre), `size` (width along the front, depth), `facing` (a bearing in degrees, or `north`, `east`, `south`, `west`), `level`, `model` (under the client's `envs/`; none: a box), `height_m` (6 for a building, 0 for a garden or field), `landmark`, `spots`. |
| `lots[].spots[]` | none | Named points (`name`, `at`): metres along the lot's front from its middle, and forward from its centre toward its front. The first number runs toward the east for a front that faces south and toward the west for one that faces north (`right = -perp(front)` on a map whose y points south): the left hand of someone standing in the lot and looking out. A spot may lie outside its lot's rectangle. |
| `resources[]` | none | Resources at authored points (`kind`, `at`, in the plan frame; anywhere on the land, not only on levels): fishing spots on a quay's edge, the trees of a copse. Optional `max` (what a node holds), `regrow_per_h` (units a game hour of growing time, turned into real time for the world's day) and `pool` (nodes naming the same pool share the first one's stock: a fishing ground); without them, the kind's `max` and `regrow_per_h` in the script. The authority places them at the nearest walkable tile when the world is created. Since `rates-2` a kind the world's tables (its script with the seed's laws) do not have refuses the seed (`floor::check`); before, it was left unplaced and logged. |
| `structures[]` | none | Structures placed when the world is created (since `rates-2`): `kind` (one the world's `structures()` has), and either `at` (the plan frame) or `lot` (that lot's door) with an optional `spot` (one of that lot's spots); `fuel` (for a kind that `burns`: the units it starts burning with, lit from creation as a seed's `structures[].fuel`) and `contents` (items it holds). Since `rules-3`, `owner: "household:<key>"` gives it to a household of the seed's residents: when the world is created it is placed after the residents, owned by the owner of that household's house (the theft law reads a store's owner). A household the residents do not have refuses the seed (`floor::check`); any other `owner` is refused by the plan's validation. Since `hearth-1` one with an `owner` and a `lot` but no `spot` stands inside that lot, not at its door ([below](#a-households-own-things-stand-inside-its-lot)). |
| `lots[].inside` | none | Since `hearth-1`: points in the frame of the lot's spots, each inside the lot's rectangle (validation refuses one outside), where the structures a household owns placed for this lot stand, in order: the first such `structures[]` entry at the first point, and so on. Without a point, such a structure takes the lot's own tile nearest its door ([below](#a-households-own-things-stand-inside-its-lot)). |
| `lots[].structure` | the lot kind's | The structure kind at the lot's door, when not its lot kind's (a barn's is a `storage`), since `rules-3`: a world's own kind of store, such as Cullet's `store`. A kind the world's `structures()` lacks refuses the seed. |
| `fixtures[]` | none | What stands in the settlement and is not a lot (since `layout-2`, [below](#fixtures-that-block)): a line (`points`, at least two, with `gaps` for its gates, each from and to in metres along it from its first point) or a rectangle (`at`, `size`, `facing`, as a lot's), `height_m` high, of a `kind` the land's `sight.hides_m` may name. One with `"blocks": true` becomes blocking tiles when the land is hosted; `eye_solid` (0 to 1) is the share of the view it blocks at eye height when it is not solid. The rules ignore a fixture that does not block. `id` is for the author. |
| `lots[].contents` | none | Items the structure at the lot's door starts with (a store's stock; since `rates-2`). A lot of a kind with no structure has nowhere to keep them: they are left out and logged. Items the world's `items()` lacks refuse the seed. |

Anything else (`wear`, `door_colour`, `keepsake`, notes for the asset generator) is ignored by the engine and is not digested, so the plan file can carry the asset generator's inputs. It is still stored: the seed's `map.params` is the world's `land.params` as written, which every client receives at connection, so what only a generator reads is better kept out of the seed.

## Authored land: the coast and stamps

A land the generator's noise cannot make (an island of a given shape, a plateau here, a beach there) is drawn with two fields of `map.params.relief` (`living_rules::land::Relief`), in land metres (x east, y south). Both are general: any land seed may use them, and a land that uses neither is unchanged.

| Field | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `outline` | none | The coastline as a polygon: the land is this shape instead of the warped ellipse round the main range. `coast_warp` (here a fraction of half the side) and `coast_feature` still roughen it. Inland, the plain rises to `lowland_m` over `outline_rise_m` (default 150) from the coast, as `tanh`. |
| `stamps[]` | none | Applied in order after the generated relief, islets and lake basins, and before the plans. Each has an `area` (polygon) and any of: `height_m` (the coarse ground inside is that height, fading into the ground round it over `blend_m`, default 40; a negative height makes shallows or a reef); `region` (a `RegionKind`: the land inside is of that kind, so its trees, beach width, rocks and dirt follow that kind's look); `ground` (`grass`, `sand`, `dirt`, `rock` or `forest`: the tiles inside are that, wherever they are not water). `id` and other fields are for the author. Lake basins keep off stamps (their area plus `blend_m`). |

`land::validate_relief`, called by the authority's `island::params_of` with the plans' checks, refuses an outline or a stamp area of fewer than three finite points, a non-finite height or a negative blend, a stamp that sets nothing, and an unknown `ground`.

What a stamp's height cannot do: it fades on every side, so a high stamp near the coast also lifts the sea floor round it (the north headland of the Wrack is a hill partly because of that). The height is the coarse (8 m) ground; the tiles' own relief (`regions.looks[].detail_m`) still rides on it.

Two things the seed already offered and Cullet needed, found while authoring it:
- A share of `regions.looks[].trees` of 0 still puts forest tiles here and there (the tile rule's noise reaches below 0); a treeless kind takes a negative share.
- `water.lakes_per_km2` of 0 still places one lake basin where the ground allows; stamps covering the land keep it off.

## What the generator does with it

1. **Relief.** `land::hydro_pass` pulls the macro relief toward each plan's ground within `blend_m` of its levels, and sinks its `sea` polygon below sea level, before the sea, lakes and rivers are found (`settle::plan::stamp_macro`). Islets and lake basins keep away from plans.
2. **Site.** Each plan is one fixed site, placed before the generated ones (which keep their usual distance from it), with `Site::plan` set, so `Site::id()` is the plan's id. Its radius holds every level, outlying ones included; unlike a generated site it does not clear forest and rock in that disc (its levels are opened by the `Plan` step), so a plan with outlying lots leaves the land between them as it is.
3. **The `Plan` step** of the planner (`settle::Staged`, only when the land has plans), right after the city step:
   - the plan's tiles: its levels are open land (water, rock and trees give way), its sea is water;
   - its ground ([`settle::plan::Ground`](../living/rules/src/settle/plan.rs) in `Land::height`'s modifications, applied before roads and pads): each level flat at its height; a smooth, steep drop inside a lower level where a higher one meets it; the outer edge blended into the natural ground over `skirt_m`, except over water, where a quay stands straight out of the sea (it falls to the bed within 0.5 m). It is continuous everywhere;
   - its streets as roads at the ground's height, ending at the square (`RoadEnd::Plaza`) where they touch it;
   - the settlement (`Settlement::planned`), its square as the plaza, its lots (contiguous, in plan order), its stairs as sloped pads and `stairs` props, and `retaining_wall` props along the drops.
4. **Everything else steps aside.** Street, lot, stair, terrace and ornament generation skip planned settlements; road profiles and road shaping leave their streets alone; their lots are their models' footprints exactly (no style margin, so terraced houses share walls with no gap). `terrace::paint_drops` makes every drop no one can step up (more than 0.8 m between neighbouring tiles) a `Drop` tile inside a plan as on terraces, except beside water: no one walks across it, everyone sees across it ([perception](PERCEPTION.md#drops-sight-across-ground-levels); before 2026-11-11 it was `Wall`).
5. **Island roads** never route through a plan's levels or harbour. When a plan has a `trunk` street, the network starts from it, and roads from the other settlements join it where it leaves the town; that is how they reach the square. Without one, the other settlements connect among themselves.

`Land::plans` (`settle::Laid`) keeps each planned lot's id, name and spots, each street's road id and surface, and the plan's authored resources on the land, for the authority. It is not digested.

## Fixtures that block

Since 2026-11-11 (branch `layout-2`). Until then a plan's walls and fences were presentation only: the kit drew them and people walked through them. A plan now says which of them block, as seed data, and hosting turns them into tiles. Nothing in it is Cullet's: `plan-test` has a walled garth, a low fence and a net rack.

```json
"fixtures": [
  {"id": "garth_wall", "kind": "yard_wall", "points": [[13, 44], [13, 53], [25, 53], [25, 44], [13, 44]], "gaps": [[36.4, 37.6]], "height_m": 2.0, "blocks": true},
  {"id": "quay_fence", "kind": "fence", "points": [[-25, 20.6], [-9.5, 20.6]], "height_m": 1.1, "blocks": true},
  {"id": "net_rack", "kind": "net_rack", "at": [-34, 21.5], "size": [3, 1.5], "facing": "south", "height_m": 2.4, "eye_solid": 0.3, "blocks": true}
]
```

- **Which tiles.** A line blocks every tile its middle crosses, point to point (`settle::plan::line_tiles`, the grid walk `Map::sight_clear` uses), so a wall that lies askew to the tiles has no leak: consecutive tiles share an edge. A gap opens the tiles whose centres, set against the line, lie inside it, and always the tile at its middle, so a gate narrower than a tile still opens one. A rectangle blocks the tiles whose centres lie in it, as a lot's footprint does. A wall is one tile thick whatever its `width_m`: tiles are a metre.
- **Foot and eye.** `host::hosted_map` blocks every walkable tile under a blocking fixture, a street's too (the author put the wall there). The tile is `Wall` where the fixture hides what is behind it and `Low` where it is seen and shot over or through ([perception](PERCEPTION.md#drops-sight-across-ground-levels), [island authority](ISLAND_AUTHORITY.md#sight-over-low-things-2026-11-11)). The rule is the land's `sight`, as for lots and props (`host::fixture_hides`):

  | A blocking fixture | Its tiles |
  | --- | --- |
  | Its `kind` is in `sight.hides_m` | `Wall` when that height is above the eye, else `Low` |
  | `height_m` is no higher than `sight.eye_m` (1.6) | `Low` |
  | Higher, with an `eye_solid` below `sight.solid` (0.8) | `Low`: a paling with gaps, a net rack |
  | Higher, solid | `Wall` |

  A fixture has no model in the rules' table, so its height and `eye_solid` are the plan's own numbers. `eye_solid` is the same measure as a model's `eye_cells` (`buildings.py` `eye_record`: the blocked share of the view in 1.2 to 1.8 m), given as one number for the whole fixture. A drop's edge or a `Low` tile under a fixture that hides becomes `Wall`; water, rock and existing walls stay.
- **Where it is kept.** `Laid.fixtures` (`LaidFixture`: id, kind, height, `eye_solid`, tiles) on `Land::plans`, in memory. It is not digested itself; the hosted tiles are, so a client that regenerates the land must agree with the authority on every wall (`host::digests`). Heights and layout digests do not move.
- **Refusals** (`settle::validate_plans`): a fixture that is neither a line nor a rectangle, or both; a line of fewer than two points or no length; a gap that does not run from a distance to a greater one; gaps on a rectangle; a negative height; an `eye_solid` outside 0 to 1; a tile off the land. After generation, hosting still refuses a plan whose building doors no one can reach (`settle::stranded_doors`), so a wall drawn across a door refuses the seed.
- **What it does not do.** No gate opens or shuts: a gap is open ground. A fixture does not change the ground's height. Building does not see fixtures as its own obstacles; their tiles are simply not open ground.
- **Lands without fixtures** are byte-identical: the island and the megacity floor keep their pinned digests (`host::tests::pinned_digests_hold`).

Tests (`cargo test -p living-rules`): `settle::plan::tests::a_blocking_fixture_blocks_walking_and_the_eye_rule_decides_sight` (on `plan-test`, turned 15 degrees: the garth's wall is `Wall`, the way in passes its gate, a wall with no gap cuts the garth off, the fence is `Low` and seen over, the rack is `Low` until `sight.solid` drops to 0.25, `hides_m` decides a kind outright, only the fixtures' own tiles differ and only the tiles digest moves), `a_lines_tiles_leave_no_leak_and_a_gap_opens_at_least_one`, `validation_refuses_a_fixture_that_is_neither_a_line_nor_a_rectangle`, and `the_cullet_seed_is_valid_and_reachable` (261 walls; every lot's spots and every authored resource still reachable).

## A household's own things stand inside its lot

Since 2026-11-11 (branch `hearth-1`). Until then every structure placed for a lot stood at its door, half a metre in front of the lot, on the street; and the authority moves every plan structure to the nearest walkable tile, so even a point inside a house came back to the street. On Cullet every lived-in house had its hearth and its household's store standing in the street at its door. The rule is general, for every planned world; nothing in it is Cullet's or a hearth's.

**Which structures.** A `structures[]` entry with an `owner` (`household:<key>`), a `lot`, and no `spot` or `at`. Everything else stands where it did: unowned structures at a lot (the Marver's lamp at its door, the smokehouses), one at a lot's spot (the Marver's public hearth on its hearth bench, plan-test's crate by the inn's seat), one at a point, and the lot's own door structure (its house, the Store), which stays the door: people live at it (`homes`), walk to it, and `house_at` finds the house by its lot row.

**Where.** On one of the lot's own tiles: a tile under its footprint (`host::footprint_tiles`; a planned lot has no margin) that is not a street's (a road left open under it), not water and not under another of the plan's lots (`settle::plan::placed_structures`, called by the authority's `island::host` on the hosted tiles):

| The lot | Where the structure stands |
| --- | --- |
| names an `inside` point for it | at that point, when its tile is one of the lot's own; else on the lot's own tile nearest it, at the tile's centre |
| names none (the default) | on the lot's own tile nearest its door that no other structure of the lot has taken, at its centre: a standable one first (an open lot's ground: rule (b)), else under the building (a house's floor, the tile behind its door: rule (a)) |

A house's tiles are all blocked, so a hearth and a store placed for it stand on its floor, on the two tiles behind the door: the first is right behind it, about a metre from the door's own tile; the second is beside it. On an open lot (a court, a yard, a garden) they stand on its ground, which people walk on. The authority puts them exactly there (`seed::put_authored` with `exact`): they are not moved to walkable ground.

**Reach.** A structure on a blocked tile is used from where a person can stand, as every structure is: an act whose target is out of reach walks first, to the nearest reachable tile to the target (`motion::start`: the first walkable tile of the walker's ground in the nearest ring of tiles round it), and then acts if it is within the act's reach plus 1.2 m (`act.rs`, a place's arrival). `nearest <kind>` in a graph and the scene's structures are by distance, not by sight (`common::structures_near`), and a structure's own tile does not hide it (`Map::sight_clear` stops before the end tile), so a hearth behind a door is found and seen as before. Hosting refuses a plan when a structure it stands inside a lot has no walkable tile of the main walkable area within 1.5 m (`settle::INSIDE_REACH_M`, the reach of cook, feed, store and take), naming it (`map.params.plans: no one can stand within 1.5 m of the household structures inside <plan>:<lot> (<kind>)`), or when a lot has no tile of its own. For the default the walk ends within 1.5 m (the tile behind the door is in the door tile's ring); a named point off a tile's centre may end up to 1.65 m away, inside the 2.7 m an act allows.

**Rows written.** None new and no schema change: the same `structure` rows (and their `inventory` rows) are inserted in the same creating transaction, after the residents, with different `x`, `y` and `chunk`; a structure's chunk follows its point, so its subscribers are those of the house's chunk. Hosting reads each owned lot's footprint once (a few tiles per structure).

**Worlds without plans are unchanged.** `placed_structures` reads only `land.plans`; a land without plans has none, the island and generated cities have no `Inside` structure, and every other caller of `put_authored` (a seed's `structures`) passes `exact: false`, so it snaps as before. verify-generation's island and megacity structure rows were compared with `origin/main`'s ([below](#verification-hearth-1)).

**For the client.** Such a structure stands inside a building on a blocked tile. Since `hearth-1` Cullet's 21 home hearths and 22 household stores stand on their houses' floors, about 0.5 m behind the front wall's face; the client draws them at their rows' points, inside the house model, where the model's front wall may cut through them (a hearth's model is 1.3 × 0.8 m). Drawing them inside the house, or hiding them while the house is drawn closed, is the client's choice; the rules do not need them seen.

## Refusals

`settle::validate_plans(plans, size)`, called by the authority's `island::params_of`, so `create_floor` and `init` refuse a bad plan before anything is generated or written. It refuses: duplicate ids (plans, levels, streets, stairs, lots); anything outside the land; levels that overlap each other or the sea; references to unknown levels; a lot off its level, on a street or on a stair; overlapping lots (sharing a wall is fine); a stair whose rise disagrees with the levels it joins, whose foot or head is off its level, or that climbs more than 1 m per metre; a square off its level; a bad `facing`. After generation, hosting refuses a plan whose building doors no one can reach on the hosted tiles (`settle::stranded_doors`), and, since `hearth-1`, one with a household's structure inside a lot that no one can stand within 1.5 m of, or an `inside` point outside its lot (`settle::placed_structures`, validation).

## The authority

The plan rides in `land.params` and the existing rows; no table, column, reducer or view changed.

- **Homes by lot.** A seed town on a plan may give `homes`: household key → lot id. Those households live at that lot's door and own its house. The others take the remaining houses, nearest the square first, as before. Without `homes`, nothing changes.
- **Named buildings at a lot.** `buildings[].lot` puts the building's sign at that lot's door, and the building is the lot's own structure when it is of the same kind (its owner set), else a structure of its kind there. Without `lot`, it is placed near the centre as before.
- **Places by lot.** A `town_life` place or `work` kind `lot:<id>` is that lot's spots (each resident takes the nearest of the least used, as for other spots), else its door. `PlannedLot.key` and `PlannedLot.spots` are in memory only. A spot is stood on as written (`town::seat` does not move it to walkable ground), so it must be a tile one can reach; since `layout-2` the rules' Cullet test and verify-generation check every spot of a plan.
- **Open lots as places** (since `layout-2`). A court, a plot or a yard is an open lot: a `lamp` lot of no height and no model, with spots at the things one works at. It costs one `lot` row and its part of `land.params`; nothing is planted or built on it. Cullet has 138 (28 before).
- **Authored resources** (`Hosted.resources`) are inserted as `resource_node` rows when the world is populated (`seed::plan_resources`, before a town's promised `resources` are topped up, so those count them), in one call or as the last step of a staged creation. A seed that wants only its authored resources of a kind sets that kind's `resource_scale` to 0, so the wild spawn adds none. Nodes naming one `pool` take the first one's id as their `pool`, as a seed's `resource_nodes` do.
- **Authored structures and stores** (`Hosted.structures`, since `rates-2`) are placed in the same step (`seed::plan_structures`): each `structures[]` entry at the nearest walkable tile to its point, lot door or spot, through the same function as a seed's `structures` (`seed::put_authored`: lit for its `fuel` from creation, holding its `contents`); each `lots[].contents` into the structure `island::build` placed at that lot's door (of the lot's own `structure` kind, when it names one). Structures a household owns wait for the residents: `seed::populate` places them after the settlements' people, with the owners `authored_households` recorded per household key. Since `hearth-1` those placed for a lot stand inside it, exactly where hosting found them ([above](#a-households-own-things-stand-inside-its-lot)).

## The game client

- A planned settlement's lot draws its own model (`model`) when it is on disk. Otherwise it is a plain grey box of its footprint and `height_m` (`island::GreyBox`, from the `lot` row's `width_m` and `depth_m`), or, for an open lot of no height (Cullet's Fish Steps, slip, Cone yard, Marver's terrace, graveyard and root plots), a 2.5 cm slab of its footprint in a darker stone.
