The expensive per-viewer rows are perceived scene copies and combat feed rows. **Where status work is charged.** The 200 ms sweeps are spread over 12 phases. Each due sweep runs and reserves its scene size plus one, then frames spend the rest of the adaptive 250–2,500-row allowance, with one oldest-due frame guaranteed. Actual row writes and CPU work can differ from that proxy. Status asks run in brain evaluation during wakes, combat cadence and regular slots. Recomputing a status spends no sweep/frame rows. The owner vitals view follows actual vitals writes and the game can recompute as time advances. B may free tick CPU and indirectly reduce missed boundaries. **Other changes on main.** R1 stores structure familiarity as ranges by actor and block, and R2 groups first sightings of remains. Both affect noticing and its evidence, outside the body-law inputs. Reply-timing's `mind_reach` is a controller-authorized procedure returning current motion, activity and perceived reach with no persisted result. These can change workload or downstream circumstances without changing the shipped `status_of` or its API. ## Overlap with battle-viewers

**Spark owns status computation. Dev-box owns per-viewer delivery.** The two designs share tick time and the comparison workload. They operate on different data, views and cost centers.

- **Frame-views.** Remote head `802116deff216758e12590a14c916d752c93e3bc` is newer than `f9d909769` and is outside the reviewed main. The original candidate used a `(viewer, near)` key. The current branch puts near copies in separate `motion_near`, `fighter_near` and `doing_near` tables and keeps far copies in the existing tables. Each tier has separate sender views backed by viewer indexes. Sender-filtered body, fighter and doing cues deliver near/engaged deltas alongside the durable copies. The tiered scheduler adds a 16-row charge per frame and bounds cue fan-out separately. Owner vitals, senses and status computation keep their current delivery paths. [Tick rate, frame-views](https://github.com/devteapot/slop-art-online/blob/802116deff216758e12590a14c916d752c93e3bc/docs/TICK_RATE.md#L425)
- **Battle-viewers B.** No standalone remote viewer branch was advertised at review time. The plan is on frame-views. Implementation follows its merge. Dev-box starts with `motion_seen`/`motion_near` behind a law, with durable table subscriptions filtered by `viewer = :sender` and the existing views during transition. Then it may move `fighter_*`, `doing_*`, `projected`/`seen_characters`, `combat_seen`/`my_combat` and `line_seen`/`my_experience`. Cue tables already use RLS. Dev-box excludes senses, status tables and `status_of`. The pinned server is 2.14.1. Dev-box's versioned analysis explains how the table deltas avoid whole-view materialization and what isolation and rollout checks the migration needs. [Battle-viewers design](https://github.com/devteapot/slop-art-online/blob/802116deff216758e12590a14c916d752c93e3bc/docs/BATTLE_VIEWERS.md#L34), [SpacetimeDB views](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/functions/views/), [subscriptions](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/clients/subscriptions/), [event tables](https://spacetimedb.com/docs/tables/event-tables/)
- **Combat-feed touch point.** `feed_row` writes perceived combat events, and `my_combat` reads them by viewer. Exact status memoization changes neither the contents, recipients, timing nor expiry of those rows. That includes combat refusals and folded witness output. Dev-box can move that stream without sequencing behind Spark. [combat.rs:99–131](../living/authority/src/combat.rs#L99), [combat.rs:197–208](../living/authority/src/combat.rs#L197), [combat.rs:798–802](../living/authority/src/combat.rs#L798), [fold.rs:88](../living/authority/src/fold.rs#L88)
- **Relative cost.** The historical 200-fighter profile reported `brain.status` at 29.2 ms/s including about 6 ms/s of diagnostics, with 268 whole-law executions/s. Dev-box's separate 120 s, 216-player-fighter run on main at about 2.8 ticks/s measured about 540 ms/s rebuilding views (`seen_characters` 257, `seen_bodies` 125, `my_combat` 74, `seen_fighters` 53) plus 109 ms/s in perception sweeps. The historical note's 633 status spans/s and its 411 cell hits plus 298 law asks/s do not reconcile as a single count. Capture aligned counters in the new profile. The old projected 10–16 ms/s saving is equivalent in magnitude to about 2–3% of the view-rebuild figure, across different workloads. It cannot be subtracted from that figure or used to project a tick rate. B is worth a bounded pass if fresh measurements confirm savings in AI evaluation. Per-viewer delivery is the larger measured cost in the dense player case. [BATTLE_SCALE.md:569–591](BATTLE_SCALE.md#L569), [Dev-box profile](https://github.com/devteapot/slop-art-online/blob/802116deff216758e12590a14c916d752c93e3bc/docs/BATTLE_VIEWERS.md#L5)

## Revised Spark sketch

1. **Profile first.** Repeat the current 200-AI-fighter baseline, starting at zero viewers. Count alarm-cell hits, whole-law calls and misses, and time facts, keying, Rhai and diagnostics separately. The historical 268 runs/s at roughly 75 µs/run put the old miss path near 20 ms/s. Treat 10–16 ms/s as a hypothesis before part lookup and assembly costs. Continue if the fresh misses justify an implementation and report any net saving with diagnostics off.
2. **Implement in rules.** Keep `Scripts::status_of` as the shared entry point. Use an optional ordered `status_parts()` declaration, the shipped eight functions, precomputed field plans, bounded caches for the active script and a full-body fallback for opaque reads. Keep a full-law rollback and an independent diagnostic that compares exact ordered results. Keep cell expiry on the active read plan and invalidate cached answers on script or mode changes. Preserve whole-law behavior for existing overlays without explicitly paired parts. This adds no database reads, writes, indexes, subscriptions or retained rows.
3. **Prove parity.** Cover threshold boundaries, missing meters and shares, night and stage, hurt-age and rate-driven expiry, cache eviction, and a stamina-only change after warm-up. Test malformed declarations, divergent explicit overlays and legacy `status_of`-only overrides. Use verify-core for in/out-of-fight alarms, comparison and rollback, verify-generation for composed floors and verify-minds for body words. Smoke-check player pills computed from owner vitals. Check exact alarm and status ordering and wording, including felt words and rethink inputs.
4. **Share baselines with dev-box.** Record the 200-fighter viewer arms at 0, 1, 4, 16 and 216 viewers, stating controllers, density, action rate, duration and subscriptions. The existing 216-player-fighter stress arm has a different controller mix from the low-viewer AI arms. Quote dev-box's baseline with that distinction. Compare status time per ask and per wall second, then tick rate and tails, view and sweep time, copy rows and messages. Dev-box owns tier/cue delivery, RLS isolation, migration and reconnect tests. Spark owns status parity and computation cost.
