# The island: world bible

The source for the island's authored people (`living/seeds/island.json`). Households and character sheets are drafted from this text and `world.json`, then checked and compiled into the seed's settlements. Everything here is history and circumstance at the moment the world begins. Nothing in it says what anyone will do next: once the world runs, what people make of their situations is theirs.

## The land

An island about two kilometres across. A mountain range runs along its eastern half: bare rock, two summits, and a few caves nobody goes into. Rivers come down from the range to the sea, and there are a few lakes. Roads join the settlements; away from the roads lie meadows, woods, old forest, high pasture and shore.

- **Brackenford**, in the north-west lowland, is the walled market town: the biggest place on the island, with a paved square, a chapel, an inn and a guildhall. Its gates are kept by the town.
- **Thornby** is a fishing village on the north shore, a short walk east of Brackenford.
- **Kettlebridge** stands on terraces above a river in the middle of the west: brick and iron, works and foundries, a clock tower in the square. It is a few hours' walk south-east of Brackenford along the stone road, which runs through woods.
- **Highshelf**, the giants' settlement, lies on a high shelf in the hills south of Kettlebridge. The giants are three times a person's height, slow-spoken and patient, and build in stone.
- **Lanternquay** is a dense harbour town of concrete blocks, neon and street stalls on the south-west coast, busy at all hours.
- **Elmwick** is a farming village in the southern woods; **Reedmoor** a small village of reed-cutters and fishers on the south-east shore.
- **Fernhollow**, the small folk's hollow, lies in the old forest in the east. The small folk are about a third of a person's height, quick, curious and fond of gardens.
- **Hamlets and steadings** lie between the towns: farms, woodcutters' and charcoal-burners' huts, a herders' fold in the hills, salt pans on the west shore, a light on the north cape.

A world day passes in about twelve real minutes and a year in eight days. Winter stops things growing, and nights are cold for anyone without a roof, a fire or a cloak. Wolves run in the hills and along the town road.

## Peoples and ways

The island's people are not one people. Each settlement has its own names, ways and faith.

- **Brackenford and the villages (Thornby, Elmwick, Reedmoor) and their hamlets** share a rustic speech and old island names: Aldric, Mercy, Wat, Agnes, Osric, Nell, Hob, Bet, Tobin, Wenna, Edda, Cuth, Marta, Ned. They keep the **Hearth**: every house keeps its fire, the chapel keeps the common fire, and a guest who has eaten at your hearth is under your roof's peace for the night. Brackenford's sisters of the chapel heal and keep the calendar.
- **Kettlebridge** speaks in the clipped, formal way of the works: Josiah, Hester, Silas, Ruth, Ezra, Ambrose, Prudence. Its people believe in work, contracts and the clock. The **Works Council** of masters governs the town; apprentices are bound to masters by written indentures, and runaways are sought.
- **Lanternquay** people use short names and harbour nicknames: Juno, Kai, Mara, Dele, Tamsin, Wick, Ostrander. They light **tide lanterns** on the quay for the drowned, and they owe nothing to anyone who has not shared a boat with them. The **Harbour Warden** speaks for the quay.
- **The giants of Highshelf** have long names of stone and weather: Hollbrenna, Tavvik, Orrum, Gaudra, Vellmund. They keep **cairns** for their dead on the ridges above the shelf; a cairn is never moved. Their elder decides for the shelf, slowly, after everyone has spoken.
- **The small folk of Fernhollow** take plant names and house names: Pipwort Bramblecap, Thistle Mossfoot, Sorrel Underbank. They keep the **Old Green**: every hollow keeps gardens, and a guest is fed before being asked anything. The Eldest of the hollow keeps its memory.

People of different settlements know of each other by trade, gossip and old quarrels. Most people have never been to more than two or three settlements.

## Money, goods and what each place lacks

People trade in goods and in **marks**, small stamped tokens worth what people agree they are worth. A household's marks are its own. Each settlement's common store belongs to its community; taking from another community's store without leave is theft in its eyes.

| Place | Has more than it needs | Lacks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brackenford | berries and grain from its fields and farms, hides, cooked meat, timber | stone for its walls and houses, fish in winter |
| Kettlebridge | stone, clay, iron work, marks | food: it does not grow enough to feed its works |
| Lanternquay | fish, cooked and fresh | stone and timber: the lower quay has not been rebuilt since the surge |
| Highshelf | stone, meat, hides | timber (few trees on the shelf) and fibre for cloth |
| Fernhollow | fibre, berries, salves | timber and stone (the small folk do not fell the old forest) |
| Thornby | fish | grain (it owes Brackenford) |
| Elmwick | berries, fibre, timber | fish |
| Reedmoor | fibre (reeds), fish | timber |

## The roads and who carries on them

- **The stone road** runs from Kettlebridge north-west through the woods to Brackenford's south gate. Kettlebridge carters bring stone and clay to Brackenford and take berries back. It passes close to the old Coldwell steading in the woods.
- **The fish road** runs from Lanternquay north to Kettlebridge: Lanternquay runners carry cooked fish up and stone back down for the seawall.
- **The giants' track** runs from Highshelf down to Kettlebridge. One giant carries meat down and timber back up, against the elder's word.
- **The east road** runs from Fernhollow through the old forest to Reedmoor and on toward Elmwick. A small-folk pedlar carries fibre and salves and brings fish back.

Brackenford charges a **gate-due** on goods brought in through its gates that were not grown inside its fields: one mark a load, or a share of the load. The due pays the gate guards who keep the town road clear of wolves and outlaws. Nobody else on the island charges one.

## History

- **The Settling.** Over generations, people came to the island by sea and settled its lowland. The giants and the small folk were here before them and say so; nobody disputes it, though few people have ever asked them about it.
- **The Charter of the Square.** Long ago the island's settlements agreed that Brackenford's square would be a market for anyone who comes in peace, on market terms set by its Market Guild. Brackenford is proud of it.
- **The Lean Year** (three years ago). Blight took the berry fields across the north. Brackenford's town store fed its own and lent grain to Thornby and to the farmers of the Coldwell steading, against their next harvests. Thornby still owes forty baskets. The Coldwell farmers could not pay; Reeve Aldric Marrow took their fields for the town store and let them to the Market Guild's tenants, the Tolleys of Ashby. The Coldwell households refused to give up their houses and were declared outlaws at the gate. They still live at Coldwell, in the woods by the stone road, and take a share from carriers who pass: by asking, by threats, and twice by force.
- **The Surge** (two winters ago). A storm surge took Lanternquay's lower quay and the houses on it. Some families went to Kettlebridge looking for work and live on its poor terrace; one went to Elmwick. Lanternquay asked Kettlebridge for stone to build a seawall. Kettlebridge sent a little, at full price.
- **The broken match** (two years ago). To seal trade between Brackenford and Kettlebridge, Reeve Aldric's son Tobin was promised to Hester Crane, daughter of Kettlebridge's foundry master Josiah Crane. A month before the wedding Tobin broke it off and married Wenna, a Brackenford weaver's daughter. Josiah took it as an insult to his house and his town. Since then the Works Council sends as much stone south to Lanternquay as north to Brackenford, and Brackenford's gate-due was raised.
- **The cairn cutting** (last year). Kettlebridge quarrymen working the ridge between Kettlebridge and Highshelf cut into a giants' cairn. The giants' elder Hollbrenna forbade the shelf to trade with Kettlebridge. The quarryman who led the cutting, Ezra Holt, says he did not know what the stones were.
- **The Lakemead.** The meadow by the southern lake between Elmwick and Reedmoor was common grazing for both. Reedmoor's headwoman let it to the Coombe family, who pay her rent in grain. Elmwick says the meadow was never Reedmoor's to let.

## World tensions at the start

These are the conditions people live with. None of them has an ending written for it.

1. **The gate-due.** Kettlebridge's carters call it robbery; Brackenford's guards are paid from it; the Market Guild says it drives trade away. Reeve Aldric keeps it.
2. **Brackenford against Kettlebridge.** The broken match and the gate-due on one side; on the other, Kettlebridge's sharp prices and pride. Most people in each town have a low opinion of the other town, and some have friends or kin there anyway.
3. **The Coldwell outlaws.** Households who lost their fields, a deserter from Lanternquay and a runaway apprentice from Kettlebridge, under Marta Hale. They want their fields back, or a pardon, or enough to live on. They can be bargained with, joined, fed or fought. Marta's sister Agnes is married to a Brackenford gate guard.
4. **The Market Guild and the Reeve.** Inside Brackenford, Mercy Abbot's Market Guild wants the gate-due dropped and peace with Kettlebridge; the Reeve and the guards want it kept. The Reeve is old, and who comes after him is not settled.
5. **Thornby's debt.** Thornby owes Brackenford forty baskets of grain from the Lean Year and pays slowly in fish. Brackenford's ledger records it.
6. **The giants and Kettlebridge.** The cairn cutting closed the giants' trade with Kettlebridge, but Tavvik still carries meat down the giants' track for timber. Kettlebridge needs the meat.
7. **The drowned quay.** Lanternquay wants stone for a seawall and blames Kettlebridge for selling it dear. Lanternquay families on Kettlebridge's poor terrace work cheaply, and some Kettlebridge workers resent them for it.
8. **The Lakemead.** Elmwick and Reedmoor both claim the meadow; the Coombes live on it and pay Reedmoor.
9. **The light on the north cape.** Old Edda keeps a light on the cape above Thornby. Thornby says she has lit it wrong on purpose to wreck boats; she says she lights it every night.
10. **Small folk and big folk.** The small folk of Fernhollow and the giants have an old friendship from before the Settling. The small folk distrust Kettlebridge's smoke and fires, and avoid Brackenford's gate-due by trading south and east.

## Guidance for sheet writers

- Write who each person is now: voice, temperament, loves and grudges, what they have and owe, what they want and fear. Do not write what happens next.
- Situations are consistent on every side. If one person owes another, both know it. If two people are courting, both know it; their families may or may not. A secret is held only by those the situation says hold it.
- People hold opinions of other settlements from their own side of the story. Give people in a quarrel reasons, not villainy: the Reeve has guards to pay; the outlaws lost their fields; Josiah lost face; the Coombes need the meadow.
- Use the culture's names and ways. Do not give Kettlebridge people Lanternquay names, or giants small-folk names.
- Children have simple sheets: family, play, one or two memories, a fear or a wish.
