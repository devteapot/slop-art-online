# Perception: what a player receives

What a player's client may read is what its character perceives now, plus its own state (docs/PERCEPTION.md). The authority works out each player's perceived set every 200 ms: creatures within its sight (day, night, or night with a torch) with a clear sight line (rock, walls and shut gates hide). The per-sender views return only those rows.

## Sub-features

- `seen_bodies` (the narrow motion row, `Moving`: no path, chunk or kind; the travel segment and the facing offset), `seen_fighters` and `seen_doing` hold the creatures the caller perceives, nothing else; `seen_characters` holds those and the caller's own living character. Since 2026-11-10 (`scale-1`) the caller's own body, fighter and doing rows are in `my_body`, `my_fighter` and `my_doing` (at most one row each), not in `seen_*`.
- `seen_characters` is what the caller perceives of each of them: the `perceived_of` projection (`SeenCharacter`: `id`, `name` when known else empty, `label`, `kind`, `stage`, `folk`, `style`, `own`, `band`, exact `hp`/`max_hp` or -1, `look`). Since `look-1-auth` it ends with `look`, the `lk_` key of the person's line for anyone seen at any distance ("" for animals and people without a look; [looks.md](looks.md)). No `controller`, `ai`, home, parents or cause. Two newcomers who were never told each other's names see each other as `a stranger`, health as a band (`seen-characters.json`). Someone the world's `perceived_of` law marks `far` (Cullet past 24 m) is labelled `someone too far away to make out`, known or not, with no name (since far-label, 2026-11-12; `scripts/far_label.py --run client-…` on any launched run: an overlay makes everyone past 4 m far; one stranger at 2 m and one at 6 m from the player, the far one then brought to 2 m and relabelled `a stranger`, all read through the SDK probe; `far-seen-*.jsonl`, `far_label.json`). Under a law that reads distance the sweep keeps what each row was projected from and projects it again only when that changed (since cullet-sweep, 2026-11-12): `scripts/sweep_rows.py --run client-…` on a run launched with `--seed cullet` drives a fixed scene (six held strangers on an open ray at 2, 10, 20, 23, 26 and 30 m; the two beside the 24 m cut cross it; one tells its name, goes to 28 m and comes back to 5 m; the player learns a technique; one is killed) and records the player's `seen_characters` rows of the scene's people after each step through the SDK probe (`sweep-seen-*.jsonl`, `sweep_rows.json`), checking the law's labels at each step; `--expect-kept` also requires `sweep kept` log lines with rows kept and none differing from a fresh projection. Run it on main's module and on a branch's (each its own `client-*` run), then `sweep_rows.py --compare A/sweep_rows.json B/sweep_rows.json`: the same rows at every step, except the player's own exact health, set aside because a joining character's maximum health comes from genes drawn afresh in each world. No `seen_*` view carries a `community` field; only minds' scenes do. `seen_fighters` carries the projected band. The appraiser-and-bystander proof is `verify-player`'s `appraise.py`.
- In sight: two players 3 tiles apart on open ground receive each other's body and character; each gets its own body in `my_body` and not in `seen_bodies` (`sight.json`, step `in-sight`).
- Beyond sight: the same players 20-30 tiles apart receive no body, character, fighter or doing row of the other (step `beyond-sight`).
- Behind a wall or rock: two players at most 4 tiles apart (within even night sight, 6 tiles), on one axis with only rock or wall tiles between, receive nothing of the other (step `behind-wall`).
- `my_characters` is the one character the caller plays now (`perception::current`): the `player` row's character while its `controller` is the caller and it is not `ai`, the dead included until the person joins again (for the death screen); nothing while the world's minds hold it after a takeover. A person with no `player` row (a world from before the table) gets the old walk, its newest living character else its newest, until its next player reducer writes the row. It was every character the identity ever had until 2026-11-10. `player` (identity → character, private) is written at `join`, when a returning person takes the character back, and by `player_character` for a person who has no row yet. `my_body`, `my_fighter`, `my_doing`, `my_vitals`, `my_experience`, `my_places`, `my_inventory` and `my_know_how` hold only that character's rows; anonymous receives none of them and no `seen_*` rows (`own-*.json`). Since `stamina-1` `my_body` and `seen_bodies` rows (`Moving`) carry `running` (the rules' gait, for any controller; `body.running`, `motion_seen.running`), and the `my_vitals` row also carries breath and focus (`stamina`, `stamina_max`, `stamina_rate`, `stamina_from_ms` and the same for `focus`, docs/STAMINA.md). The suite checks each player's row is within its caps, with no row for an anonymous client (`own-*.json`, `my_vitals_rows`). `my_experience` holds the newest 64 lines of the living character, or of the last one while none lives (since 2026-11-10; it was every character's 400-line window).
- `my_senses` (since 2026-11-11, branch `rules-senses-outcomes`; docs/PERCEPTION.md "My senses") holds what the caller's living character senses: `CharacterSenses` (`id`, `sight`, `hearing`, `field`, `focal_deg`, `side_deg`, `side_share`, `near_m`, `margin_deg`, `at_ms`), the `senses` law's answer written at the player's frames only on change. It reads `character_senses` (private) by its key. The own-state check requires exactly one row, the caller's own character's, with sight and hearing above 0; `ClientAnon` gets its own and anonymous none; `character_senses` is in the private inventory (refused to anonymous and players). That the row follows nightfall and a law change, and is not rewritten while nothing changes, is `verify-player`'s `senses.py`.
- `my_earshot` (since `earshot`, 2026-11-12; docs/COMMUNICATION.md §17) holds, while the world's `earshot` law is on (on as shipped since `earshot-on`), one row for the caller's living character from the private `character_earshot` table: `ways` (per way of speaking in the rules' order, `way` and `hearers`, the ids of the people in the caller's scene whom a line in it would reach, nearest first) and `text`, a short form in the player's own labels (`earshot::short_words`, since `earshot-short`: "Within earshot: Ife Okoro, a stranger and 4 others; a call would also reach 3 more"); a mind's scene keeps the full words. It is written at the player's frames only when it changed (`frame::show_earshot`, at most every 250 ms), and goes when the law is off or the character dies. The suite's own-state check holds it to the caller's character and to people in its `seen_characters`. `scripts/earshot.py --run client-…` on any launched run, with the law as shipped (on): A at 3 m at say, B at 10 m added at call by day, C at 15 m (unseen) never listed, the short words reading "Within earshot: <A>; a call would also reach 1 more" by day and no word of C's name; a said line and a call reach exactly the listed among the three, and C the call; the row goes when the law is turned off and comes back when it is turned on again (`earshot-*.jsonl`, `earshot.json`).
- `my_known_names` (since 2026-11-12, branch `owner-known`; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Whose a thing is, and what places are called"): `KnownName` (`id`, `name`) for everyone the caller's current character knows by name (itself, parents, children and their other parent, its community, introductions). Read by index from `character` (parents), `acquaintance` (by observer) and `membership` (by community). The own-state check requires the caller's own character among them and names on every row; anonymous gets none. A client names a structure's owner from it with the world's `owner_shown` law. The private `land_plan` (the land's full parameters, with names) is in the private inventory; the public `land` row's `params` carries none (verify-core `scenes.py owners`).
- `my_act_outcomes` (since 2026-11-11, branch `rules-senses-outcomes`; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Act outcomes") holds how the caller's current character's acts and death came out: `ActOutcome` (`id`, the experience line's; `observer`, `at_ms`, `kind`, `ok`, `skill`, `code`, `what`, `why`, `other`). It reads `act_outcome` (private) by its `observer` btree index. The own-state check requires every row's `observer` to be the caller's character; anonymous gets none; `act_outcome` is in the private inventory. The codes, retention and the death row are `verify-player`'s `outcomes.py`.
- `my_offers` (since 2026-11-11, branch `rules-offers`; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Offers") holds the standing trade offers addressed to the caller's living character (`incoming` true) and the one it made (`incoming` false): `MyOffer` (`id`, `incoming`, `from`, `to`, `give_item`, `give_qty`, `want_item`, `want_qty`, `at_ms`, `until_ms`), character ids and no names. It reads `trade_offer` (private) by its `to` and `from` btree indexes. The own-state check requires every row's `from` or `to` to be the caller's and `incoming` exactly when `to` is; the offers step (after `in-sight`) has `ClientPrimary` offer 1 berries for 1 wood to `ClientSecond` with `human_act` and probes each identity: the maker reads it outgoing, the addressee incoming, `ClientAnon` and anonymous nothing (`offers.json`, `offers-*.json`). A player accepting it is `verify-player`'s `offers.py`.
- Cues and tiers (since frame-views, 2026-11-18; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Cues and tiers"): `seen_bodies`, `seen_fighters` and `seen_doing` each have two tiers, `seen_*_near` (the others within 8 m of the caller at its last sweep) and `seen_*_far` (the rest), reading one key each: the near tier's own tables (`motion_near`, `fighter_near`, `doing_near`, private) and the old copy tables, now the far tier; no creature is in both and their union is the untiered view's rows. The three public event tables `seen_body_cue`, `seen_fighter_cue` and `seen_doing_cue` carry what a near or engaged creature's copy would hold now, as it changes; row-level security (`viewer = :sender`) gives a client only its own, an anonymous connection none, and a query naming another identity none. `scripts/cues.py --run client-…` on any launched run (build the probe first) joins three players with their own identities (A and B 1 m apart, a held stranger F 10 m from A, C 40 m or more away), streams A's views, tiers and cues, B's, C's and an anonymous connection's cues for 28 s while B steps, waves at A and strikes A until the authority has accepted 5 strikes (at most 10 tries) and F is moved, and checks: every cue names its receiver; C and the anonymous connection get none of A's, B's or F's; B's query `WHERE viewer = 0x<A>` gets nothing; A is cued B's motion and each of B's accepted strikes' windups (its `skill_use` rows, owner SQL; at least 3, so a loaded host's refused or late strike does not fail the count: since `cues-count`, 2026-11-20) first by a cue, before any tier row of it, the cue written within `MAX_TICKS` (2) ticks of `act_ms` (its own `at_ms`, in the tick spacing the private `clock` row shows over the scene); F is never cued; B is in A's near tier and F in its far one; the tiers partition each view; and replaying A's stream by the client's rule (a cue stands until the creature's next tier row) ends on the tiers' rows. It turns the `cue_transport_on` law on for that phase (on by default since acceptance), then off: no cue reaches A, B or an anonymous connection, and B's accepted strikes' windups (at least 3) reach A by its near tier; it puts the law back as the world had it (`cues-*.jsonl`, `cues-off-*.jsonl`, `cues.json`). The checks are ticks and order, so host load does not fail them; how long a windup took to reach A's client (receipt against `act_ms`, wall clock, by cue and by the near tier with the law off; the old bounds were 100 and 150 ms) is a measurement in `cues.json` `measure` with the tick spacing and the 1-minute load, for a series to judge under a declared load (since `genesis-followups`, 2026-11-20). It is the merge scene gate's case `cues` (`living/tools/scene_gate.py`), with `scripts/near_tier.py`: the near tier holds at most `frame::NEAR_MAX` (32), whoever is engaged with the player first (a second player waving at it from 10 m is near at once, past the cap, and its next sweep ranks the tier: it and the 31 nearest of 33 strangers within 7.5 m), then the nearest within 8 m (`near_tier.json`).
- Motion copies as a table (since battle-views, 2026-11-19; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Motion copies as a table"): with the `motion_tables_on` law on (on by default since bv-defaults-on, 2026-11-20), a player's motion copies are rows of the public `seen_body_copy` (`viewer` indexed, `body`, `near`, `seg`: the view's `Moving` without its id; indexes `(viewer, near)` and `(viewer, body)`; no primary key, so a change arrives as a delete and an insert, and the probe streams it with insert and delete events only), filtered per client by row-level security (`viewer = :sender`), instead of `motion_seen`/`motion_near`; a viewer's copies are all public or all private, and `seen_bodies`, `seen_bodies_near` and `seen_bodies_far` read whichever holds them. The law off moves every public copy back in one tick. `scripts/motion_tables.py --run client-…` (build the probe first): three players with their own identities (placed with A and B 1 m apart, a held stranger F 10 m from A, C 40 m or more away; checked as B within 1.5 m, F 9–12 m, C 30 m or more) and an anonymous connection stream the table from their connections on while B steps and F is moved; checks that every row names its receiver, C and anonymous receive none of A's or B's, B's subscription and SQL naming A's identity get none, C's subscribe-all by name and `SELECT * FROM *` get no one else's, every probe's subscription is accepted, A's table equals its `seen_bodies` rows and its `near` flags its tier views' split, and the private tables hold none of A's copies; then the law off: the table empty to the owner within a second, A's stream of it ends empty, A's views show the same creatures from the private tables; on again, A's rows return (`motion-tables-*.jsonl`, `motion_tables.json`). The scene gate's case `motion_tables`. `drive.py` fails when the set of row-level-security rules changes (`RLS_TABLES`), and when the mind service or the observer names `seen_body_copy`.
- Projections mirrored as a table (since battle-views, 2026-11-19; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Projections mirrored as a table"): with the `characters_table_on` law on (on by default since bv-defaults-on, 2026-11-20), each of a player's `projected` rows is also a row of the public `seen_character_copy` (`id` the projected row's, primary key; `viewer` indexed; `row` the `SeenCharacter` the view returns), filtered per client by row-level security (`viewer = :sender`); `perception::proj_*` are `projected`'s only writers and keep the mirror whole per viewer; the law off drops every mirror row in one tick. `scripts/motion_tables.py` covers it with the motion copies (both laws on, then off and on again through `law_override` rows): every mirror row a player receives names it, C and anonymous get none of A's or B's, B's subscription and SQL naming A's identity get none, subscribe-all both ways gets no one else's, A's mirror ends on its `seen_characters` rows, the law off empties it within a second with the view unchanged, on again it returns, and neither switch reinstalls the script (27 checks; without the mirror's rule 7 fail). `drive.py`'s `RLS_TABLES` and owner guard name `seen_character_copy`.
- Fighter copies as a table (since views step 3, 2026-11-20; docs/PERCEPTION.md "Fighter copies as a table"): with the `fighter_tables_on` law on (off by default), a player's copies of the fighters it perceives are rows of the public `seen_fighter_copy` (`viewer`, `body`, `near`, `fighter`), row-level security `viewer = :sender`, instead of the private `fighter_seen` and `fighter_near`; all or none per viewer (`copy_state.fighters`), moved whole at its frame and back in one tick when the law goes off; `seen_fighters` and its tiers read the public rows when the viewer has any. `scripts/motion_tables.py` proves it with the motion copies and the mirror (34 checks; without the rule 5 fail), and `battle_viewers.py --privacy --fighter-tables` at battle density.
- Both copy tables at battle density (since copy-privacy, 2026-11-20; docs/BATTLE_VIEWERS.md "Addendum 3"): `living/tools/battle_viewers.py --privacy --motion-tables --character-tables --law motion_tables_on=true --law characters_table_on=true` (build `gate-load` release and the probe first) on a battle world of its own (`verify-lookcost-*`). `gate-load` checks every copy row each viewer receives against its own identity over the whole run (initial rows, inserts, updates, across deaths and rejoins). - Frames (since 2026-11-10, docs/PERCEPTION.md "Frames"): others' rows in `seen_bodies`, `seen_fighters` and `seen_doing` are per-viewer copies (`motion_seen`, `fighter_seen`, `doing_seen`, private) that the tick writes at the viewer's frame, every 33–100 ms; the caller's own body, fighter and doing rows are read directly. Experience lines are released at the character's frame (`line_seen`, private). The four tables and `player` are in the driver's private inventory.
- In a `scoped` build the tables behind these views are private to anyone but the database owner, players included (`visibility.json`). In a `LIVING_PERCEPTION=open` build (diagnostics only; the default is `scoped`) they are public, so the views are correct but are not the boundary there.

## How to get to it (user POV)

A player connects with its identity, joins, and subscribes `SELECT * FROM seen_bodies` and the other views. As its character moves, the rows of creatures coming into and going out of its sight arrive as inserts and deletes; a perceived creature's steering updates arrive as updates at the player's next frame (within 100 ms).

## Driving it with drive.py

The fixed suite runs it after the combat and town checks:

```bash
LIVING_PERCEPTION=scoped .agents/skills/verify-client/scripts/run.py --run "$R"
```

`drive.py` finds the geometry on the world's own map (`terrain_chunk`, public): an open pair, a far point, and an opaque run between two walkable tiles. It places `ClientPrimary` and `ClientSecond` with the admin `place_at`, waits 1 s (five sweeps) and probes each player's `seen_*` views. `sight.json` holds the spots, each step's positions and what each player received.

## Gotchas

- The geometry search assumes the realm map has a thin rock or wall run (gap 1-3 tiles) somewhere; on a map without one the check fails with "no sight-check geometry", which is a fixture failure, not a product one.
- `drive.py` records which build it drives from the run's state (`perception`, set from `LIVING_PERCEPTION` at launch). Run the suite on both builds when a change touches visibility: `scoped` (the default) proves the boundary, `open` proves the diagnostic build still makes those tables public and the views still answer there.
- The sweep runs every 200 ms and frames every 33–100 ms; a probe right after `place_at` can still see the old set. The driver waits 1 s.
- Since 2026-11-09 the combat feed (`my_combat`) and minds' percepts see by the same rule (sight and a clear sight line): a blow behind a wall no longer reaches a player's feed. `verify-minds`'s `sight` case drives the minds' side.
- A room line's `my_experience` speech row says how it was said in the player's own labels (since `speech-address`, docs/COMMUNICATION.md §16): "A stranger said to the room, naming a stranger and you: “…”" while the player knows neither, "naming <K>" once K has told their name, and "… while talking with <K>" after the speaker's line to K. No name the player does not know is in it (`scripts/room_address.py --run client-…` on any launched run, read through the SDK probe; `address-seen.jsonl`, `room_address.json`).
- `my_experience` rows of a character the world's minds controlled while its person was away carry the mind service's identity as `controller`; the own-state check accepts the caller's identity or the world admin's.

## The field of view

Since 2026-11-11 (`fov-1`; docs/PERCEPTION.md, "The field of view (seventh change)"). `drive.py`'s `field_of_view` runs after the wall checks: two players 5 m apart on open ground (`sight_spots`' `field` pair, with open rows beside it), the admin's `set_world_law fov_on true`, and the primary player turning with the `face` act (`human_act`). Each look is a real subscription to `seen_*` and `heard_sounds` with the player's token.

- Facing away: the other is in neither `seen_bodies` nor `seen_characters`; `heard_sounds` holds a row to the east (bearing 2), near (band 0), kind `person`, with only `key`, `kind`, `bearing`, `band` and a key that is neither player's id.
- Facing it: seen, and no longer heard to the east.
- The field's edge: kept at 75° off facing once seen (through 45°), not seen afresh at 75° (through 100°).
- Off again (`set_world_law fov_on false`, then the 2 s linger): seen all round, nothing heard.

`heard`, `hear_salt` and `law_change` are in the private inventory (`PRIVATE`; `heard` and `law_change` also refused to a player), `heard_sounds` in `VIEWS`, and `set_world_law` in `ADMIN` (refused to players and anonymous callers with "admin only"). The probe reports `heard_sounds` in its `perception` map (`key` as a string).

## Whose a house is, with its owner away (house-owner-label)

Since 2026-11-12 (docs/PERCEPTION.md "Whose a thing is"). A client names a structure's owner from the public `structure` row's `owner` and `my_known_names` (`living/game/src/looks.rs` `structure_name`), neither of which reads where anyone stands, so a client's house labels never depended on the owner's position. A player's character is told no "You notice …" lines (`brain.rs` `noticed` returns for a character that is not `ai`), so the owner-labelled text a player does receive from the authority is a witnessed deed such as a theft ("A stranger took 1 berries from <F>'s storage", `my_experience`), which did read the owner's distance: "… from someone too far away to make out's storage" when the owner was away under a far rule. `scripts/house_label.py` drives both: `house-known.jsonl`, `house-structures.jsonl`, `house-lines.jsonl`, `house_label.json` (checks, the lines, and how the client's rule reads the storage).

- Known gap, for the client (not fixed here): the client ignores `shows_owner` (no shipped kind sets it) and a world's own `owner_shown` law; `house_label.json`'s `client_reads_storage_as` is "storage" where the player's line says "built by <X>". Showing it needs the kind's trait from the compiled script and the owner's name for an owner not in `my_known_names`, which no player row carries today.
