# Spring-near validation

Follows [2026-11-19-0942-flicker-2](2026-11-19-0942-flicker-2.md). Branch `spring-near` from `origin/main` `2a5cdfdfe`. The client's spring-bone scheduler (`living/game/src/characters.rs` `springs`) now simulates only characters whose full (near) mesh is drawn. Before, it simulated every visible character within the height-scaled 40 m bounds, up to the nearest 48. The far model has no spring bones, so that work produced nothing on screen. A character whose full mesh comes back is settled first, so its chains don't swing in. This is a client-only change: no authority, table, reducer or subscription changes.

## Change

- **Who is simulated:** a visible character whose `lookdev::Lod` shows the full mesh (`far_now != Some(true)`), or one with no far model (VRoid bases). The near-model cap (8, the player included) bounds the count. The existing 48 / 40 m bounds stay as the outer limit.
- **Order:** `springs` now runs `.after(lookdev::far_models)` in PostUpdate. The LOD decision and the simulated set are taken in the same frame, so a newly shown full mesh is drawn with its settled chains in its first frame.
- **Far → near:** the existing re-entry path places the tails at rest (`fresh`, as the budget re-entry did). It then takes `LIVING_GAME_SPRING_SETTLE` steps (default 60) against the frame's pose, with drag at least 0.4, and drops the velocity (`prev = tail`). The hanging pose does not depend on drag. `SETTLE=0` is the old start from rest.
- **Near → far:** simulation stops. The joints go back to `r0` while hidden, and the next entry settles again.
- **Diagnostics:**
  - `LIVING_GAME_PERF=springfar` brings back the old scheduler, which is used as the "before" arm below.
  - `LIVING_GAME_PERF=springwake` logs each woken character's largest joint turn per frame for its first 30 frames (`springwake:` lines).
  - The `springs:` line now ends with `N characters a stepped frame`, the number simulated.
- **Docs:** `perf.rs` doc lines, `docs/GAME_BRIEF.md` (Springs) and `features/crowd.md` (`springs`).

## Cost: verify-ui camera-lod fixture, Ours fixed 58°

- **Fixture:** Brackenford street, 17 placed residents, 1600×900, uncapped frame rate, 8 s per stop.
- **Measures:**
  - *spring CPU a frame* is the `springs:` line's CPU time summed over threads, per frame.
  - *a 60 Hz step* is the CPU time of one fixed step for everyone simulated.
  - *simulated* is the characters per stepped frame.
- **Arms:** the quieter pass is pass 3 (before) against passes 1 and 2 (after), run back to back.

### Reading

- **The count:** with the cap, the simulated count drops from 21–24 to 8, the near count.
- **The cost:** the spring system's CPU falls by about 55–60%, from about 9–10 to about 4 µs a frame, or about 32 to 13–17 µs a fixed step.
- **Frame time:** p50/p99 do not move measurably, because springs were already a fraction of a percent of a 4.7 ms frame. This is not a performance-contract claim: one laptop, a 17-person street, shared GPU, and the load reported above.

### With the coming hair bundle

The bundle adds front-lock chains to the 88 `long*` looks (88 of the book's 1,655 lines, 5.3%), at about 3.3 µs a person-step:

| Simulated | Expected added (5.3% long-haired) | Worst case (all long-haired) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| After: at most 8 | 8 × 0.053 × 3.3 ≈ 1.4 µs a step | 8 × 3.3 = 26 µs a step, ≈ 40 µs a step all told (≈ 12 µs a frame at about 210 fps) |
| Before, this street: 22 | 22 × 0.053 × 3.3 ≈ 3.9 µs a step | 22 × 3.3 = 73 µs a step, ≈ 106 µs all told |
| Before, at the 48 budget | about 8.4 µs a step | 158 µs added, ≈ 230 µs a step at the measured 1.5 µs a character-step |

After the fix, the bundle's worst case is bounded by the near cap instead of by the crowd within 40 m.

## Hand-off

### Fixture

- **Placement:** one resident placed 3 m in front of the player and turned to face the camera, with the LOD cap off.
- **Switch:** thresholds raised so the switch falls between zoom 0.6 and 1.0, where a person is 85–140 px tall: `LIVING_GAME_LOD_FAR_PX=18.5`, `LIVING_GAME_LOD_NEAR_PX=20.5`. With the client's projection, a 1.75 m resident's head is 16.45 px at zoom 0.6 and 24.1 px at 1.0.
- **Crossings:** three each way, 8 notches each. After each the camera holds still for 2.5 s.
- **Recording:** 30 fps (`LIVING_GAME_RECORD`).

### Residents

- **Resident 5** (`lk_00d11055…`): an adult man in a smock_74 skirt, `swept.3` hair, 12 spring joints. Five drives.
- **Resident 6** (`lk_58629a10…`): a woman in a braid with apron and smock skirts, 17 joints. Four drives.

One earlier street drive (cap 8, real thresholds) never switched resident 5, because placed residents took the eight slots. Two drives with misjudged thresholds never crossed. They are not counted. Each drive's first wake (residents still being placed at the scene's start) and `lk_0122…` (walking, 45° either way) are left out of the table below.

- **With settling:** the chains go from rigid to hanging within the switch frame, while the full mesh replaces the far model (which was drawn rigid), and are then still.
- **Without settling:** the same 20° arrives as a swing over about ten frames. That is the swing-in the fix removes.
- **Resident 5:** this skirt's groups hang at their rest direction (no gravity), so it shows nothing in any arm.
